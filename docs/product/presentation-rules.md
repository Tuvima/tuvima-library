# Current Presentation and Product Rules

These rules describe current, product-owner-approved behaviour. They were moved out of the always-loaded `CLAUDE.md` so that sessions read them only when working on the affected surfaces. Engineering ownership rules for the Dashboard live in `src/MediaEngine.Web/CLAUDE.md`. When rules conflict, the most recent dated section wins.

## Grouping, cards, Home, and detail pages

Current product rule: a Series is the lane-level shelf shown in Read, Watch, or Listen. A broader Universe/Collection is shown on `/collections` only when a shared series/franchise/universe relationship connects multiple shelves. Watch has separate `TV Shows` and `Series` shelves: TV show identities never share the movie Series row, while Series contains only movie groups dynamically aligned from trusted library metadata. TV shows may appear as show cards with one or more owned episodes, but non-TV/non-music series cards require at least two distinct owned works by default (`lane_group_display.*.minimum_series_items`). TV Timeline groups a show by its canonical premiere year, never the newest owned episode year; Network grouping renders the resolved network logo in the same identity position used for person-led group cards. A single owned movie/book/audiobook remains a normal item tile in its respective lane; audiobooks belong in Listen, not Read. A lane shelf such as owning multiple Matrix films stays in Watch without duplicating itself as a top-level Collection. Multiple formats of one work are variants, not collection triggers. Internal collection IDs may back a lane group, but user-facing surfaces must name and route by the group type: a TV show is a show, a book sequence is a series, and a curated rollup is a collection/list. Book, comic, and movie series cards preserve structural order while selecting at most four representative preview items; broader collections prefer cross-media breadth. Non-TV series and collection containers render through the fixed-size landscape `MediaGroupTile`: rest is artwork-led, with two to four slightly angled, overlapping images using actual portrait, square, or wide metadata. Home and Discover hover preserve the exact artwork and dimensions, add the purple boundary/glow, and reveal only a lightly shaded compact top overlay for type, title, and one status line. Direct grids instead embed an all-caps title, year, and owned count inside the bottom of the card; hover adds the glow and a small open cue without a type pill or cinematic replacement. There is no permanent copy column, child-title list, carousel, or child-level navigation. The retired artwork carousel and rotating collage must not be restored. The entire group card opens the group. TV shows remain on `MediaTile`; cinematic backdrop, logo, facts, and description hover is limited to Home and Discover. Existing item tiles remain on `MediaTile`.

Every card is one semantic link to its detail surface and contains no inline Read, Play, Listen, My List, reaction, remove, or details controls. Vertically wrapping `MediaTileGrid` results preserve the exact resting cover or group artwork on hover and keyboard focus, show a compact title and optional year below the art, and add only a stronger purple glow—no hover identity strip, scrim, background replacement, or popover. Direct browse cards omit redundant media/group pills such as `TV Show` inside the TV Shows route because the active navigation already supplies that context. Their filter areas provide a tile-size slider that changes width while retaining portrait, square, and landscape ratios; Music defaults smaller than the other formats. Movie and TV cinematic landscape expansion is reserved for Home and lane Discover shelves when background artwork supports it; its left-aligned rating, classification, year, and runtime row uses a compact translucent backing that hugs the text, with no inline actions. Touch never shows a hover preview or hover strip: a tap opens details, and a tile whose title only the preview carried (cinematic Movie/TV cards) shows its compact title and year under the art instead. On phones shelf rows run edge to edge with a 16 px gutter and cards are sized from the screen width, about 2.6 portrait or 2.4 square cards across and landscape series, collection, group and Continue Watching cards at 86% of the screen (at most 340 px, 320 px for Continue Watching), so the next card always peeks and no card is wider than the screen.

**Phone Home shelves.** Continue Across Media is one horizontal row on phones (tablets and desktops keep their per-type groups): newest activity first, started items only (an item never started is not in it), each card with its art, title, a thin progress bar only when there is real progress (never at zero), a small type icon and one line saying where you are (S1 E2 · 23 min left, Chapter 4, 42% read). A tap opens the card's normal destination. When nothing has been started the whole section, header included, is not shown. Every Home shelf header (Recently Added, Watch, Read, Listen) reads title on the left and See All on the right as a 48 px target. The Recently Added filter chips (All, Watch, Read, Listen, View) stay in one row and scroll sideways instead of wrapping. Items still being matched keep their Still being matched tag, are drawn smaller and quieter, and sort after the matched items.

Home alone uses `CinematicHeroCarousel`/`CinematicHeroSurface` as a landing treatment; detail heroes continue sharing `DetailHeroContent`, and both use `HeroBackdrop` for foreground cover/album geometry and book decoration. Home uses an approximately 80svh desktop feature (72svh on short screens), with bounded cover-led composition for unknown, portrait or undersized sources; measured landscapes use the aspect-band framing policy. Phones and short landscape screens use content-sized composition. Detail heroes retain `95svh`. Opposite edge arrows have 48px targets; the centered active dash is thicker, wider, and purple. Focus, hover, pause, and reduced motion suspend rotation. On touch the hero also moves with a horizontal swipe of more than 48 px (less vertical travel), and rotation is held while a finger is down. Home has no lane submenu. Its rows are Continue Across Media, Recently Added, then populated Watch, Read, Listen, and Collections & Lists; Fresh is retired. Recent filters All/Watch/Read/Listen/View retain the selected scope in `/recently-added?type=` and use added-at keyset paging. View contributes only independently authorized Mine assets, with fresh render-time media grants and native viewer navigation. Untouched TV features use show art; started Home TV keeps the show title, shows `Sx Ey · Episode Title`, and uses the exact owned episode still/synopsis/action with show-art fallback. Watch discovery remains rooted in show art. Shared explicit subject/state/episode contracts drive Engine spotlight selection, Continue, and sequence detail. Only partial long-form media has a purple artwork-edge progress strip; albums, tracks, and View omit completion bars. Resting movies/TV/books/comics use portrait frames, albums and audiobooks use square frames, and only Continue Watching uses landscape stills. Artwork is contained when its native ratio differs from its frame. Home shelves share one artwork height (on phones about 2.6 portrait cards across); only Home Watch discovery cards expand horizontally in their row; Watch browse grids, episode lists, and non-Watch cards remain outline-only. Cards retain one semantic detail link and partial long-form artwork-edge progress, without detached percentage captions. Read, Watch, Listen, and Collections retain their compact shared route navigation, viewport-anchored desktop rails, unboxed scoped filters, and existing downstream browsing behavior. On phones the filters on Watch, Read, Listen, Collections and Search collapse into one *Filters (n)* button that opens a bottom sheet holding the same controls (with Clear and a sticky *Show N items* button; filters still apply live), while the browse-mode strip scrolls sideways with a fade edge and the card-size slider is labelled in words (*Card size: Medium*); tablets and desktops keep the inline filter panel. With no hero, Home content starts below the top bar. A tile for an item still being matched or enriched shows a small soft dot before its caption title, with the tooltip "Still being matched"; the dot does not pulse under reduced motion. Where a tile shows a hover card, Movie/TV cards use the landscape banner layout only when real landscape art exists; otherwise the portrait art layout.

**Phone Home hero (under 600 px).** Phones replace the full-bleed carousel with `PhoneHeroCard`: one rounded, inset card whose height is the same for every slide and media type (about 55% of the space between the top bar and the bottom bar), so the first shelf header never moves and always sits above the bottom bar. The card has fixed slots: picture, title (two lines at most), one meta line, one full-width primary button (48 px at least) and at most two 48 px icon buttons (My List, More; Rate and the thumbs live in More). The picture ladder is a landscape backdrop with the title logo over it when there is one, then the cover at its own shape (it only ever shrinks), then a tinted placeholder tile that names the media type. The Engine's stand-in cover is a 100 by 150 picture with no flag of its own, so a cover whose measured width is unknown or under 240 px counts as no artwork and gets the tile, never a stand-in drawn at its natural size; phones ask for the sized (`m`) rendition. Nothing rotates by itself: no timer, no arrows, no pause button; swipe and the labelled dots change slides, and the dots are real buttons inside a region with an accessible name. An item still being matched gets the placeholder (or its picture) plus a small "Still being matched" tag. The meta line comes from `PhoneHeroPresentation.MetaLine` (never raw decimal minutes: 101 min reads 1h 41m; right plurals; empty or zero parts hidden; movie year, runtime, rating; TV year and seasons, an episode S2 E6 and year; book author, year, pages; album artist, tracks, time; audiobook author, length). The title sits in a fixed two-line logo zone: a logo (however wide or tall) scales to fit inside it and a title with no logo is drawn as text in it, so the card never changes height. The primary button reads by type from the data the Engine already sends: unstarted Play (movie), Watch S1 E1 (TV, from the episode or the Engine's label), Start Reading (book, comic), Listen (audiobook), Play album (music); started Resume S5 E1 with the time left (movie, episode), Continue Reading with the percent read, Continue Listening with the time left. Continue, Resume and "0%" never appear on an unstarted item, a time or percent suffix is shown only when the Engine supplied the number, and the button stays one 48 px line with an ellipsis. An album always plays from its first track because the hero carries no track position. The hero shows no synopsis: the text zone is full and a synopsis would either cover the picture or change the card height. Tablets and desktops keep the carousel above.

Canonical media, person, series, and standard-collection details use a full-width `95svh` desktop cinematic stage so its lower navigation remains visible while native 16:9 artwork loses as little of the frame as possible. Edge-to-edge landscape art is pinned top-center beneath the translucent global app bar and translucent lower navigation; the bottom is deliberately darkened more heavily than the top so the source's upper composition and hero copy remain legible. Music albums use the shared hero and lower navigation: the cover supplies a blurred atmosphere, a large sharp album/record composition is centered in the right half, identity and actions grow upward from the shared lower-left anchor, a compact Play button and circular Shuffle/My List/Rate/More controls share the action row, and MusicAlbum exposes **Overview** plus **Details**. Overview uses a proportional Tracks/Credits layout, keeps the borderless list with Show missing and no search, resolves identified artists into the same compact square clickable people cards used by movie and TV credits, and conditionally shows every other owned album from the exact canonical primary artist in a full-width **More by** shelf. Oversized provider box-set manifests are scoped to the canonical disc that represents the tagged local album; ordinary multi-disc albums retain their full sequence. Technical and source content belongs only to Details. Audiobooks retain the shared detail structure, chapter-level playback, and specialized player tools, use a substantially larger cover where viewport height permits, and keep Restart in More alongside the other shared actions. Audiobook Chapters remain embedded; Editions does not return, and lists contain neither Play All nor a second My List action. Structural details place their ordered sequence, collection, or owned-works array below the stage on Overview only, ending with an owned summary and an authoritative-total progress bar when available. Collections use a neutral no-backdrop hero and the same up-to-four representative artwork cluster as collection tiles at a dominant scale; they suppress contributor credits, expose only a functional Shuffle action, and use a compact row for the year range, total items, and applicable Read/Watch/Listen counts. Books and comics share a larger cover-only foreground envelope, as do poster-only movie/TV heroes; true landscape backdrops remain edge-to-edge. People use the same title, role-credit, facts, and synopsis typography as other detail pages, keep only the portrait treatment person-specific, and render one continuous page without tabs. Linked identities reuse cast/credit portrait cards. Explicit collective pseudonyms are the displayed and attributed author identity; linked real identities are not promoted unless explicitly credited on another work. Real multiple-author bylines retain order and use semicolons followed by one space. Person music credits collapse tracks into one album card and show roles/year without track counts. Role and lane filters render only when more than one real option exists. Utility actions use larger icon-left-of-label controls on a left-growing row beneath the primary action. Jump to appears beside the array heading only above ten entries; alternate series/arc selectors align left and Show missing aligns right. Person works use managed portrait covers/posters or square album art, never cinematic landscape backgrounds, and prefer owned-asset canonical titles over parent collection labels. Overview combines attributed description or biography, a purpose-built cast/credits peek, and applicable series or collection context; Detail tabs per type: movie, TV show and season **Overview, Cast, Details**; episode **Overview, Cast, Details**; album **Overview, Details**; audiobook **Overview, Tracks, Details**; book and comic **Overview, Credits, Details**; series types and Collection **Overview, Details**; a Universe page **Overview** (with the owned items in it) and **Details**; a person one page with no tabs. Cast shows each actor with the role name they played, and links to the person. There is no Character page, no Characters, Portrayals or Relationships tab and no View Appearances button; character and place links across formats belong to the Universe plan. No detail page has a Universe or Related tab, on any device; the Universe explorer and Collections stay reachable from their own places, and a stale `?tab=universe` or `?tab=related` link falls back to the first tab. **Details** reads top to bottom: file facts, then History. File facts are whatever the data store already holds per item (file name and format, container, length, pages, video, audio and subtitle summary, language, and per file the size such as `1.4 GB` or `312 MB`, the date it was added such as `2026-10-16`, the bitrate such as `6.2 Mbps` or `256 kbps`, and the audio and subtitle tracks when more than the summary already says); everything comes from stored rows and nothing is probed on page open, so a fact that is not stored is simply left out, shown on every device as a compact two-column list that wraps on phones, with one block per file when an item has several; groupings (shows, seasons, albums, series, collections) own no file and point to the items inside. The file path and History appear only to people who are already offered the Edit action, and History is a read-only list shown only on desktop-class screens, read from `GET /library/items/{id}/history`. Standard collection details use the same resolved membership as the Collections catalog. Listen playlists remain specialized lane-local surfaces. On phones the Listen song lists (All songs, album and artist songs, playlists) and the album details track list become card lists: each song shows its title, then artist and album, its length on the right, a 44px Favourite heart and a row menu (a bottom sheet) with Play next, Add to queue, Go to album and Go to artist; a playlist adds Move up, Move down (disabled at the ends) and a destructive Remove. Tapping a card plays it, and long song lists load 120 songs at a time behind a *Show more songs* button. Nothing on touch depends on drag or hover: drag handles and column resize grips appear only with a mouse at the desktop width, a playlist's rows reorder from the row menu on touch and with Alt+Up / Alt+Down on a focused row on desktop and TV (the move is announced), and the row menu button is always visible on touch. Below 1024px the now-playing bar's title block and transport share the width instead of holding fixed widths. On phones (the phone form factor) the hero is sized to its content under the top bar instead of filling the screen: the primary action is full width and 48px high, My List, Rate and More are 44px round icons with their names underneath, fact rows wrap as chips, genre chips keep a 44px tap box, and More opens a bottom sheet listing the same actions. The section tabs keep a 112px minimum width, fade at the edge that still has tabs and scroll the active tab into view; the episode and works rails swipe with the next card peeking, credits and people stay in one or two columns, and track rows fit the screen without sideways scrolling. Tablet and desktop keep the composition above.

A detail page never shows a button that goes nowhere: the main button exists only when there is something to play or read (Watch, Resume, Read, Listen, Play album, Explore Timeline), so a page with nothing playable, such as a track with no readable or listenable format, a show with no owned episode or a universe without a timeline, has no main button and no generic "Open" fallback. Every action the Engine sends either carries a route to open or is one the Dashboard handles itself (`DetailActionKeys`), and a test fails when one does neither.

## Collections presentation

Current presentation rules: the Collections landing route is labeled **Discovery**, while Automatic, Curated, Shelves, and People retain their names. Shelves opens with Read, Watch, and Listen preview lanes and filters by Books, Movies, and Audiobooks. Albums and comic issue series remain native lane groups. Same-name duplicate contributor records collapse into one enriched shelf presentation. Discover rows show a small title and optional year for individual items, but group tiles do not repeat text they already embed. TV Shows rows keep episode counts but omit the redundant `TV Show` pill. Completed TV shows show a provider-backed premiere-to-finale year range; TV detail pages always keep the season selector visible, including when only one season is owned.

A curated collection (a manually built Custom collection) can never be empty. The editor does not let it be created or saved without at least one item and sends the chosen items with the create request; the Engine refuses to take out its last item ("A collection needs at least one item. Delete the collection instead.") and refuses an update that would leave it empty. When the last item of a curated collection leaves the library the collection is deleted automatically and a "Removed the collection 'X' because its last item left the library." entry is written to the activity history. Playlists, playlist folders and rule-driven (live) collections are not affected, and automatic collections follow the canonical metadata. Warning before deleting a library item that sits in a curated collection belongs to the future "Delete from library" work.

## Series rails and app shell

Canonical book, comic, and movie series containers show their sequence rail directly on Overview. Source numbering stays above each cover, connectors appear behind number nodes only between proven consecutive positions, and the current item uses a stronger purple frame glow without `This book`, `This movie`, or `Up next` labels. Completion remains a separate check state, and `aria-current` preserves accessible current-item context. Missing-item visibility inherits its media default from `config/ui/library-preferences.json`; the database stores only explicit profile-and-series overrides, which can be removed to restore config inheritance.

A standalone work (no collection, no series value and no parent) never shows a series or sequence panel on its details page. Opening any details page from a browse surface starts at the top, and Back restores the previous page's scroll position.

`MainLayout` exposes My List as the active profile's saved shortlist and delegates account actions to `TopNavAccountMenu`. Needs Review lives inside that permission-aware menu rather than in a standalone bell. `SystemActivityIndicator` uses `ShellActivityState` to combine playback, ingestion, AI download/parsing, enrichment, and durable-operation activity into one circular progress surface, using success green for its icon and ring while work is active and hiding when idle. The account menu offers Sign out for the current session, including local accounts. Below 600 px (the phone form factor) navigation is the three-tab floating dock, the hamburger menu and the section chips described under *Phone navigation* below. Tablets and desktops show the top navigation and no dock; TV uses the five-tab dock (Home, Libraries, Search, Downloads, Settings) from `shell.intent_dock_items`, where **Libraries** goes to the library surface the profile used last (Watch until one has been used; remembered per profile in browser storage under `tuvima.last-library.v1.<profile id>`), is active on Watch, Read, Listen, View, Collections and Recently added, and, tapped while already on one of those surfaces, opens the library switcher (a bottom sheet of six 56 px rows: Watch, Read, Listen, View, Collections, Recently added). **Search** opens the full-screen search overlay (on phones safe-area aware, with a visible Cancel button, the on-screen keyboard's search action, and results as a one-column list of tappable rows). **Downloads** opens `/downloads`, which says downloads live in the Tuvima app; `ShellDock.DownloadsMode` switches between that page and dropping the tab. The older names Read, Watch, Listen, View and Collections stay valid in the TV list so saved settings still resolve. A Kids profile (restricted role) gets the simpler Kids shell: the top bar keeps only Watch, Listen and Read and My List, the phone dock is the same three tabs as everyone's (the phone menu and chips list only Home, Watch, Listen and Read, with no Settings or Recently added; the TV switcher lists only Watch, Listen and Read and has no Settings tab), tiles are larger with rounder corners (`.layout-kids` tokens in `wwwroot/app.css`), the account menu drops Profile settings, Settings and Needs Review and shows a *Kids* badge. Content limits are separate and not part of the shell.

The top-bar My List button uses the list-with-tick icon (`PlaylistAddCheck`), which supersedes the ringed-planet glyph in the October 6 2026 policy below; the bookmark shape is reserved for real bookmarks. On details pages, My List is a "+" action labelled Add to My List, which becomes a plain tick "✓" labelled In My List once saved. The account menu header shows the account email. The profile switcher appears only with more than one profile, and the menu includes Manage profiles (administrators) and Secure account (this-computer-only accounts, which also show a dot on the avatar). There is no bar across the top of the screen for this-computer-only accounts: the prompt lives in the account menu, Settings > Security (see below) and the "Add a password to your account first" toast.

## Phone navigation (under 600 px)

> **Reversal.** This replaces the five-tab phone dock (Home, Libraries, Search, Downloads, Settings, with no hamburger and the Libraries switcher sheet) that earlier revisions of this page described. The Libraries tab, the Downloads tab and the Settings tab are gone from the phone dock; the library switcher and the five-tab dock remain on TV only. Tablet, desktop and TV navigation are unchanged.

The phone shell has four parts, all on the phone form factor only (`html[data-form-factor="phone"]`):

- **Top bar.** A fixed 56 px bar row: a 48 px **hamburger** on the left, the icon-only logo (`tuvima-icon.svg`, no wordmark), then on the right the Downloads icon (see below), My List, library activity and the account avatar. There is no search icon: Search is a dock tab, so there is one Search. The bar floats over the Home and details heroes with the same translucent gradient and blur as before, and is a solid surface on other pages.
- **Section chips.** A fixed 48 px row under the bar (so the whole top chrome, `--app-topbar-height`, is 104 px): **Read, Watch, Listen, View, Collections**, in the order the desktop top bar uses, scrolling sideways with a fade at the edge that still has chips. The chip for the section the person is in is selected (`aria-current="page"`, accent border and soft fill, and it scrolls into view). The row has one tab stop and the left and right arrow keys, Home and End move between chips. Held sideways (under 480 px tall) there is no room, so the row is dropped and the menu still lists every section.
- **Floating dock.** A rounded pill of three 48 px tabs, the same for everyone including Kids: **Home**, **Search** (opens the full-screen search overlay) and **My Library**. It sits clear of the screen edges and the bottom safe area, has its own grid row so it never covers the last row of a page, and sits below the audio player. **My Library** opens the My Library page (`/my-library`, described below) and is selected there and on the personal grids behind its See All links (`/for-me`); the details-page **+ My List** action keeps its name.
- **Menu.** The hamburger opens a full-screen sheet (a native modal dialog: focus stays inside, Escape closes it, the browser's Back also closes it and returns to the page, and focus goes back to the hamburger; it only animates when the person has not asked for reduced motion; every row is 56 px tall and labelled). It is generated from the same catalogue as the desktop top bar (`ShellNavigation`, so it cannot drift): **Home**, **My Library** (the dock tab's page), **Recently added**, then the top bar lanes in the top bar's order (Read, Watch, Listen, View, Collections), each with its icon. Photos and Documents are not separate entries: they live inside View. Below a divider: **Settings**, **Switch profile** (only when the household has more than one profile) and **Sign out**. Every entry appears only if the active profile may see it, using the same rules as the desktop bar and account menu, so a Kids profile sees Home, My Library, Watch, Listen and Read and Sign out only, and a profile that may not see Documents never meets the word in the menu, the chips or the dock.

The **Downloads** icon sits on the right of the top bar only where downloads actually work, which is the Tuvima app. The browser Dashboard cannot download, so `ShellDock.DownloadsMode` ships as `ExplainNativeApp` and the icon is hidden there; `DownloadsTabMode.Available` shows it.

On Home the phone hero card is sized from the chrome above and the dock below (`--phone-hero-bottom-bar` in `PhoneHeroCard.razor.css` is the one number that sets it), so adding the chips row did not move the first shelf header; the card is correspondingly shorter than when the bar was 65 px with no chips.

## My Library page

My Library is the personal page, named like My Netflix. It combines Continue, My List and the other personal sections in one responsive column that phones show first; the desktop and tablet top bar is unchanged (its My List button lands on the same page). Route `/my-library`; the heading and the browser tab title say My Library. Old `/for-me?view=my-list` links land on it. The grids behind See All stay on `/for-me`: **My List** opens `/for-me?view=my-list&area=all` and **Liked** opens `/for-me?view=liked` (a link that carries an area, such as the Read, Watch and Listen My List entries, still opens the filtered grid).

Top to bottom, each section hidden when it has nothing to show:

1. **Profile header.** Avatar, My Library and the profile name, a **Switch profile** button (only when the household has more than one profile) and a **Settings** link (never for Kids). No account editing.
2. **Downloads card.** A link to `/downloads`, shown only where downloads work (`ShellDock.ShowsDownloadsInTopBar`, that is `DownloadsTabMode.Available`, the Tuvima app). It is not rendered at all in the browser.
3. **Continue.** Every media type in one row, using `PhoneContinueLogic` and the phone Continue row (tablets and desktops keep the per-type groups). Source: `GET /display/continue`.
4. **My List.** Saved items, newest first, with See All. Never hidden: when empty it keeps its title and says "Add titles with the + My List button", with no buttons. Source: the saved ids resolved through the Engine's direct item lookup (`POST /display/items`, see *Personal pages resolve ids directly* below).
5. **History.** Items the profile finished watching, reading or listening to, most recently finished first. Source: `GET /display/continue?finished=true`, the cards the Engine marks Completed, ordered by their last-accessed time. Music has no finished state, so a started album stays in Continue.
6. **Liked.** Everything marked Like or Love, newest first, with See All. Source: profile reactions resolved through the same item lookup.

Kids profiles get the same sections, narrowed by the Engine's existing permission and content rules (every read is profile-scoped). A profile that may not see Documents never meets a Document item (they are hidden until the View settings say Documents are allowed). There are no editing controls on the page.

**Loading.** The header loads first, then every section loads in parallel. Until the header is in, only the heading and a header skeleton show. Each section holds a fixed-height skeleton (Continue about 285 px and every shelf 341 px at 390 px wide, the same height as the loaded row) and sections reveal top to bottom, so a section that turns out empty can only move skeletons below it, never content that is on screen. Artwork is the sized renditions only. A section whose read fails is left out and one quiet notice with Try again appears after the last section. On phones every shelf header, including this page's, is one 48 px row with the title on the left and See All on the right.

**Personal pages resolve ids directly.** My Library and For Me turn the saved, liked and favorite ids into tiles with `POST /api/v1/display/items` (`PersonalTileIndex.LookupAsync`), not by reading the Read, Watch and Listen lane browse pages, so an item is never missing just because it sits past a lane page's size. The Dashboard sends at most 200 ids per call (the Engine's cap) and splits a longer list into several calls, one page back. The Engine answers from the same authorized projection as the lane pages, so the profile's content limit and the account's lane and library grants apply exactly as they do on a lane page; an id the profile cannot see, a Collection or Playlist id (those come from the Collection catalogue and Playlist reads) and an id that does not exist all simply have no card. Documents are never returned, so a profile that may not see them cannot learn that one exists, and the Dashboard still hides any Document tile it is handed. Collections and Playlists keep their own reads. If the lookup or the finished-items read fails, that section is left out and the quiet notice appears, rather than the row showing as empty.

## TV form factor, focus and spatial navigation

The tv form factor (`html[data-form-factor="tv"]`, no layout redesign) is a larger, remote-friendly presentation of the same pages. One block in `wwwroot/app.css` sets an 18px root font size, 56px dock targets with 14px labels, one step larger tile titles, a 48px shelf gutter and a 60ch maximum line length for hero subtitles and synopses. Phone and desktop layouts are unchanged. Every focusable control (tiles, dock items, chips, buttons, table rows, hero controls) shows one shared focus ring on keyboard or remote focus: the `--tl-focus-ring` colour with `--tl-focus-ring-width` and `--tl-focus-ring-offset`; text fields keep their own field focus styling. Spatial navigation (`registerSpatialNav` in `wwwroot/app.js`) moves focus with the arrow keys by on-screen position: left and right stay within a shelf, up and down move between shelves, the dock and the hero. Enter activates the focused control, Escape or Backspace go back, and the focused tile scrolls into view. It is always on in the tv form factor; on other form factors it starts after the first arrow key pressed while focus is on a shelf tile. It never acts inside text fields, selects, sliders or editable regions, or while a dialog is open.

## TV episode routing and heroes

TV episodes are children of the show detail page, organized by season. A combined file (one file, several episodes) is named with its episode run when the organiser renames files, for example `s01e01-e02`; every episode it covers is shown as owned. Each owned
episode has a show-scoped detail route opened from its still. Continue surfaces
retain the episode still and playback target, identify it with compact copy such
as `Continue · S5 E1`, and use actions such as `Resume S5 E1`. Episode detail
heroes retain that episode's still, synopsis, and genre. Root show heroes report
owned episodes and target either the in-progress episode or the first owned episode.

## Shared editor and Universe graph rules

- Treat structural media placement, authored-container canonical title/order, and the knowledge graph as separate models. - **Editing is desktop-only.** One rule, `EditorDeviceRule.CanEdit`, decides: the form factor is desktop AND the browser reports a fine, hover-capable primary pointer (`pointer: fine` and `hover: hover`). Phone, tablet, TV, a native app shell, an unknown form factor and a browser that cannot report its pointer all mean no, so an iPad asking for the desktop site cannot edit while a touch laptop with a mouse can. Phones, tablets, TVs, cars and native apps are consumption-only in the Dashboard: no pencils, no Edit button, no drawer and no Dashboard edit routes. The Review Queue stays a desktop workflow (phones keep its summary only, and the launcher refuses if a review editor is reached elsewhere).

The state lives on the page: it ends on Done or when the page is left, and a deep link never starts in Editing Mode. There is no undo and no change journal; a single-field edit saves straight away and can simply be edited again, and History stays read-only. ### Who's using Tuvima? (October 9 2026)

`/who` is a full-screen page with no main navigation. It shows the signed-in account's profiles (its grants inside its own household, never another household's) as a Netflix-style grid: avatar or initial, name, a lock tag when the profile has a PIN and a *Child* tag for restricted profiles. Picking a profile switches to it; a profile with a PIN always asks for that PIN first (this is what stops a child profile switching into an adult one), and a wrong PIN counts toward the lockout only for people connecting from outside. Below the grid, *Always open as this person on this device* remembers the choice for this browser or device only; *Stop always opening as Mary* clears it.

The page appears straight after a password, passkey or provider sign-in when the account has more than one enabled profile and this device has no saved choice, and from the account menu's *Who's watching?* item (shown only with more than one profile). The same menu lists the other people in the household for a one-tap switch: a person with a PIN opens the number pad inside the menu, and a wrong PIN, a lockout or a refusal is shown there with nothing changed. The list reuses the profiles the page already loaded, so opening the menu adds no Engine requests. An account with one profile never sees it. When the saved person has a PIN, sign-in opens the page with that PIN prompt instead of skipping it. Administrators in a household that has a Child profile and an adult profile without a PIN see one gentle note: *Set PINs on adult profiles so children can't switch into them.* Nothing is blocked. Restricted profiles still never reach admin areas.

### Manage profiles on the picker (October 10 2026)

Administrators (server or household) see a *Manage profiles* button under the grid, and an *Add profile* tile while the household has fewer than eight people. Kids (restricted) profiles never see either, and nobody else does: the Engine only lets administrators change people. *Manage profiles* turns the grid into edit mode: every face shows a pencil, tapping one opens the profile editor sheet (name, avatar, Kids, content limit, PIN, delete) instead of opening the profile, and *Done* returns to normal. The page also opens in this mode at `/who?manage=1`, and with the Add profile sheet open at `/who?add=1`; both stay on the page even for a one-person household.

Adding creates a household person (optionally a Kids profile and a PIN) and then uploads the chosen photo. Saving an existing person changes name, colour and icon first, then the PIN (set, change or remove), then the photo (a new photo, or removal when a colour or icon replaces it). A Kids profile can't be switched to an adult one afterwards. Delete is offered for everyone except the last profile, the Owner profile and the profile that is open right now. Deleting asks what to do with the person's personal photos: keep them in the household's Shared Library (the default, in a folder and tag named *From <name> <date>*; managed files move, linked originals are copied and left where they are) or delete them. Habits (likes, My List, progress, bookmarks, taste picks) are always removed and library files are never touched. If any photo can't be kept, nothing is removed. Every change needs a recent sign-in (the usual *Confirm it's you* prompt) and, when administrator protection is on, the administrator PIN, which the page asks for once and then repeats the change.

### Content limit per profile (October 10 2026)

Every profile has its own *What can this person watch?* setting, chosen by an administrator in the profile editor: Everything, G, PG, PG-13 or R. It is separate from the Kids label, so a parent can allow an eight-year-old PG-13 or limit an adult. A new Kids profile starts on PG, everyone else on Everything. The editor shows the choice as a five-step segmented control with a one-line explanation, plus a *Show items with no rating* switch (off by default, shown only while a limit is set) because music, personal videos and some imports carry no rating.

The editor sheet is compact: a large avatar preview with one *Change* button that opens a Colour / Icon / Photo sub-panel (collapsed on open, closed again after a colour or icon is picked; the "replaces the current photo" note shows only on the Photo tab). Kids profile, content limit, the unrated switch and PIN share one row style (label and one-line hint left, control right, at least 44 px tall); the PIN row shows *No PIN* or *PIN set* with Set / Change / Remove buttons and opens the PIN pad inline only while changing. It is a bottom sheet on phones and a centred dialog about 560 px wide elsewhere.

The Engine enforces the limit, not the screens. Ratings from every vocabulary (film G to NC-17, TV-Y to TV-MA, UK and plain ages such as 12, 15, 18, and book or comic labels such as Everyone, Teen, Mature) are placed on one ladder; anything above the profile's step, and anything unrated unless allowed, is removed before a list, search, Home shelf, collection, Continue Watching row, detail page, file, stream or player queue is built. A TV episode takes its rating from the episode, then its show. A change applies on the person's next request. A limited item is reported as not found. Changing the limit needs the same sign-in checks and administrator PIN as any other profile change.

## Book reader highlights, bookmarks and settings (`?reader=new`)

- **Tools.** The top bar of the new reader has Contents, Bookmark this page, Bookmarks and highlights, and Reading settings. Each opens one panel: a side panel on tablets and desktops, a bottom sheet on phones (about three quarters of the height at most), closed by the scrim, its Close button or Escape.
- **Highlights.** Selecting text shows a small toolbar under the selection with five colours and "highlight and add a note". Tapping an existing highlight opens a card with its colour, its note, Delete and Done; closing the card saves a changed note. Highlights are saved per profile, anchored by a standard EPUB position (CFI range), so they stay on the same words when the text size or width changes. Highlights made in the retired reader are not shown. Devices without text selection (a TV remote) can still see highlights and use bookmarks, contents and settings.
- **Bookmarks.** "Bookmark this page" saves the current place with the section name and percent as its label; a place that is already bookmarked says so instead of adding a duplicate. The list opens a bookmark, newest first, and deletes it.
- **Settings.** Theme (light, dark, sepia, auto), text size, line spacing, typeface (the book's own, serif, sans), page width (narrow, standard, wide) and layout (pages or one scroll). They apply immediately. The reader opens with the profile's reading defaults (`/settings/reading`), setting by setting: a setting the person changed in the reader on this device wins over the profile value, and a setting never changed on this device uses the profile value (so a later change to the profile still reaches it). A change in the reader is remembered on the device and also saved to the profile a moment later, and only the setting that changed is written to either. **Reset to my defaults** in the reader's settings panel clears this device's choices, including any saved before this feature, and shows the profile's values again.
- **Keyboard and remote.** Arrow keys, Page Up/Down and Space turn pages while the page has focus; while a toolbar, panel or card has focus, the arrow keys are left to it so a remote can move between controls. Enter or OK on the page shows or hides the bars. Escape closes the card, then the panel, then leaves the book.
- **Phones and TVs.** Bars respect safe areas, panels become bottom sheets under 600px, and controls grow on screens 1600px and wider.
- **Nothing spills past the page.** On reflowable books every element is held to its page column: wide tables and fixed-width blocks shrink to fit, long unbroken words and dotted leaders wrap, and `white-space: nowrap` is set back to normal, so a printed contents list on a front page never leaves a clipped continuation on the next page. Fixed-layout books are left exactly as published. `scripts/visual-qa/book-reader/run-checks.mjs` measures this at 390, 820, 1280 and 1920 px.
