# Current Presentation and Product Rules

These rules describe current, product-owner-approved behaviour. They were moved out of the always-loaded `CLAUDE.md` so that sessions read them only when working on the affected surfaces. Engineering ownership rules for the Dashboard live in `src/MediaEngine.Web/CLAUDE.md`. When rules conflict, the most recent dated section wins.

## Grouping, cards, Home, and detail pages

Current product rule: a Series is the lane-level shelf shown in Read, Watch, or Listen. A broader Universe/Collection is shown on `/collections` only when a shared series/franchise/universe relationship connects multiple shelves. Watch has separate `TV Shows` and `Series` shelves: TV show identities never share the movie Series row, while Series contains only movie groups dynamically aligned from trusted library metadata. TV shows may appear as show cards with one or more owned episodes, but non-TV/non-music series cards require at least two distinct owned works by default (`lane_group_display.*.minimum_series_items`). TV Timeline groups a show by its canonical premiere year, never the newest owned episode year; Network grouping renders the resolved network logo in the same identity position used for person-led group cards. A single owned movie/book/audiobook remains a normal item tile in its respective lane; audiobooks belong in Listen, not Read. A lane shelf such as owning multiple Matrix films stays in Watch without duplicating itself as a top-level Collection. Multiple formats of one work are variants, not collection triggers. Internal collection IDs may back a lane group, but user-facing surfaces must name and route by the group type: a TV show is a show, a book sequence is a series, and a curated rollup is a collection/list. Book, comic, and movie series cards preserve structural order while selecting at most four representative preview items; broader collections prefer cross-media breadth. Non-TV series and collection containers render through the fixed-size landscape `MediaGroupTile`: rest is artwork-led, with two to four slightly angled, overlapping images using actual portrait, square, or wide metadata. Home and Discover hover preserve the exact artwork and dimensions, add the purple boundary/glow, and reveal only a lightly shaded compact top overlay for type, title, and one status line. Direct grids instead embed an all-caps title, year, and owned count inside the bottom of the card; hover adds the glow and a small open cue without a type pill or cinematic replacement. There is no permanent copy column, child-title list, carousel, or child-level navigation. The retired artwork carousel and rotating collage must not be restored. The entire group card opens the group. TV shows remain on `MediaTile`; cinematic backdrop, logo, facts, and description hover is limited to Home and Discover. Existing item tiles remain on `MediaTile`.

**Videos (Watch).** Videos is the plain Watch kind (nothing is looked up) for courses, talks and downloads; it can hold several named libraries. Personal and phone footage belongs in View's Personal videos, not here. Its tab on Watch shows standalone videos and series (a folder of videos, with optional sections for sub-folders) as cards. A series opens the show page and a standalone video opens the movie page, so Resume, Up Next, progress and Mark watched work as they do for TV. A Videos folder is read from its layout and local files only (YouTube ID in the name or `.info.json`, `.nfo`, then the cleaned file name); the YouTube lookup (YouTube kind only) sends only the video ID. Saved videos have no ratings, so they follow each profile's "allow unrated" setting like every other unrated item; a folder marked "Suitable for limited profiles" also shows its unrated videos to profiles with a content limit. An item or series marked Explicit is never shown to such a profile.

**Browse tabs and Unmatched (Watch, Listen, Read).** Each lane shows one tab for every kind that has folders (Movies, TV Shows, YouTube, Music, Audiobooks, Books, Comics) and one for every named custom library (for example Lectures), in that order; a kind or library with no folders has no tab, and a person only sees the tabs for libraries they may see. Each library tab has an Unmatched filter chip that anyone who can see the library may use; it lists that library's files the Engine could not match, flat, with no grouping. Administrators get Retry now, Keep as is and Look up again in an Unmatched item's "..." menu on its page; Keep as is asks first and the item stays under Unmatched, marked Kept (and out of the Unmatched count), until it is looked up again. Unmatched files never appear in the lanes, on Home, in shelves or in collections, and Search lists them in a separate "Unmatched" group, found by file name and tags, never mixed into the matched results.

Every card is one semantic link to its detail surface and contains no inline Read, Play, Listen, My List, reaction, remove, or details controls. Vertically wrapping `MediaTileGrid` results preserve the exact resting cover or group artwork on hover and keyboard focus, show a compact title and optional year below the art, and add only a stronger purple glow—no hover identity strip, scrim, background replacement, or popover. Direct browse cards omit redundant media/group pills such as `TV Show` inside the TV Shows route because the active navigation already supplies that context. Their filter areas provide a tile-size slider that changes width while retaining portrait, square, and landscape ratios; Music defaults smaller than the other formats. Movie and TV cinematic landscape expansion is reserved for Home and lane Discover shelves when background artwork supports it; its left-aligned rating, classification, year, and runtime row uses a compact translucent backing that hugs the text, with no inline actions. Touch never shows a hover preview or hover strip: a tap opens details, and a tile whose title only the preview carried (cinematic Movie/TV cards) shows its compact title and year under the art instead. On phones shelf rows run edge to edge with a 16 px gutter and cards are sized from the screen width, about 2.6 portrait or 2.4 square cards across and landscape series, collection, group and Continue Watching cards at 86% of the screen (at most 340 px, 320 px for Continue Watching), so the next card always peeks and no card is wider than the screen.

**Phone Home shelves.** Continue Across Media is one horizontal row on phones (tablets and desktops keep their per-type groups): newest activity first, started items only (an item never started is not in it), each card with its art, title, a thin progress bar only when there is real progress (never at zero), a small type icon and one line saying where you are (S1 E2 · 23 min left, Chapter 4, 42% read). A tap opens the card's normal destination. When nothing has been started the whole section, header included, is not shown. Every Home shelf header (Recently Added, Watch, Read, Listen) reads title on the left and See All on the right as a 48 px target. The Recently Added filter chips (All, Watch, Read, Listen, View) stay in one row and scroll sideways instead of wrapping. Items still being matched keep their Still being matched tag, are drawn smaller and quieter, and sort after the matched items.

Home alone uses `CinematicHeroCarousel`/`CinematicHeroSurface` as a landing treatment; detail heroes continue sharing `DetailHeroContent`, and both use `HeroBackdrop` for foreground cover/album geometry and book decoration. Home uses an approximately 80svh desktop feature (72svh on short screens), with bounded cover-led composition for unknown, portrait or undersized sources; measured landscapes use the aspect-band framing policy. Phones and short landscape screens use content-sized composition. Detail heroes retain `95svh`. Opposite edge arrows have 48px targets; the centered active dash is thicker, wider, and purple. Focus, hover, pause, and reduced motion suspend rotation. On touch the hero also moves with a horizontal swipe of more than 48 px (less vertical travel), and rotation is held while a finger is down. Home has no lane submenu. Its rows are Continue Across Media, Recently Added, then populated Watch, Read, Listen, and Collections & Lists; Fresh is retired. Recent filters All/Watch/Read/Listen/View retain the selected scope in `/recently-added?type=` and use added-at keyset paging. View contributes only independently authorized Mine assets, with fresh render-time media grants and native viewer navigation. Untouched TV features use show art; started Home TV keeps the show title, shows `Sx Ey · Episode Title`, and uses the exact owned episode still/synopsis/action with show-art fallback. Watch discovery remains rooted in show art. Shared explicit subject/state/episode contracts drive Engine spotlight selection, Continue, and sequence detail. Only partial long-form media has a purple artwork-edge progress strip; albums, tracks, and View omit completion bars. Resting movies/TV/books/comics use portrait frames, albums and audiobooks use square frames, and only Continue Watching uses landscape stills. Artwork is contained when its native ratio differs from its frame. Home shelves share one artwork height (on phones about 2.6 portrait cards across); only Home Watch discovery cards expand horizontally in their row; Watch browse grids, episode lists, and non-Watch cards remain outline-only. Cards retain one semantic detail link and partial long-form artwork-edge progress, without detached percentage captions. Read, Watch, Listen, and Collections retain their compact shared route navigation, viewport-anchored desktop rails, unboxed scoped filters, and existing downstream browsing behavior. On phones the filters on Watch, Read, Listen, Collections and Search collapse into one *Filters (n)* button that opens a bottom sheet holding the same controls (with Clear and a sticky *Show N items* button; filters still apply live), while the browse-mode strip scrolls sideways with a fade edge and the card-size slider is labelled in words (*Card size: Medium*); tablets and desktops keep the inline filter panel. With no hero, Home content starts below the top bar. A tile for an item still being matched or enriched shows a small soft dot before its caption title, with the tooltip "Still being matched"; the dot does not pulse under reduced motion. Where a tile shows a hover card, Movie/TV cards use the landscape banner layout only when real landscape art exists; otherwise the portrait art layout.

**Phone Home hero (under 600 px).** Phones replace the full-bleed carousel with `PhoneHeroCard`: one rounded, inset card whose height is the same for every slide and media type (about 55% of the space between the top bar and the bottom bar), so the first shelf header never moves and always sits above the bottom bar. The card has fixed slots: picture, title (two lines at most), one meta line, one full-width primary button (48 px at least) and at most two 48 px icon buttons (My List, More; Rate and the thumbs live in More). The picture ladder is a landscape backdrop with the title logo over it when there is one, then the cover at its own shape (it only ever shrinks), then a tinted placeholder tile that names the media type. The Engine's stand-in cover is a 100 by 150 picture with no flag of its own, so a cover whose measured width is unknown or under 240 px counts as no artwork and gets the tile, never a stand-in drawn at its natural size; phones ask for the sized (`m`) rendition. Nothing rotates by itself: no timer, no arrows, no pause button; swipe and the labelled dots change slides, and the dots are real buttons inside a region with an accessible name. An item still being matched gets the placeholder (or its picture) plus a small "Still being matched" tag. The meta line comes from `PhoneHeroPresentation.MetaLine` (never raw decimal minutes: 101 min reads 1h 41m; right plurals; empty or zero parts hidden; movie year, runtime, rating; TV year and seasons, an episode S2 E6 and year; book author, year, pages; album artist, tracks, time; audiobook author, length). The title sits in a fixed two-line logo zone: a logo (however wide or tall) scales to fit inside it and a title with no logo is drawn as text in it, so the card never changes height. The primary button reads by type from the data the Engine already sends: unstarted Play (movie), Watch S1 E1 (TV, from the episode or the Engine's label), Start Reading (book, comic), Listen (audiobook), Play album (music); started Resume S5 E1 with the time left (movie, episode), Continue Reading with the percent read, Continue Listening with the time left. Continue, Resume and "0%" never appear on an unstarted item, a time or percent suffix is shown only when the Engine supplied the number, and the button stays one 48 px line with an ellipsis. An album always plays from its first track because the hero carries no track position. The hero shows no synopsis: the text zone is full and a synopsis would either cover the picture or change the card height. Tablets and desktops keep the carousel above.

Canonical media, person, series, and standard-collection details use a full-width `95svh` desktop cinematic stage so its lower navigation remains visible while native 16:9 artwork loses as little of the frame as possible. Edge-to-edge landscape art is pinned top-center beneath the translucent global app bar and translucent lower navigation; the bottom is deliberately darkened more heavily than the top so the source's upper composition and hero copy remain legible. Music albums use the shared hero and lower navigation: the cover supplies a blurred atmosphere, a large sharp album/record composition is centered in the right half, identity and actions grow upward from the shared lower-left anchor, a compact Play button and circular Shuffle/My List/Rate/More controls share the action row, and MusicAlbum exposes **Overview** plus **Details**. Overview uses a proportional Tracks/Credits layout, keeps the borderless list with Show missing and no search, resolves identified artists into the same compact square clickable people cards used by movie and TV credits, and conditionally shows every other owned album from the exact canonical primary artist in a full-width **More by** shelf. Oversized provider box-set manifests are scoped to the canonical disc that represents the tagged local album; ordinary multi-disc albums retain their full sequence. Technical and source content belongs only to Details. Audiobooks retain the shared detail structure, chapter-level playback, and specialized player tools, use a substantially larger cover where viewport height permits, and keep Restart in More alongside the other shared actions. Audiobook Chapters remain embedded; Editions does not return, and lists contain neither Play All nor a second My List action. Structural details place their ordered sequence, collection, or owned-works array below the stage on Overview only, ending with an owned summary and an authoritative-total progress bar when available. Collections use a neutral no-backdrop hero and the same up-to-four representative artwork cluster as collection tiles at a dominant scale; they suppress contributor credits, expose only a functional Shuffle action, and use a compact row for the year range, total items, and applicable Read/Watch/Listen counts. Books and comics share a larger cover-only foreground envelope, as do poster-only movie/TV heroes; true landscape backdrops remain edge-to-edge. People use the same title, role-credit, facts, and synopsis typography as other detail pages, keep only the portrait treatment person-specific, and render one continuous page without tabs. Linked identities reuse cast/credit portrait cards. Explicit collective pseudonyms are the displayed and attributed author identity; linked real identities are not promoted unless explicitly credited on another work. Real multiple-author bylines retain order and use semicolons followed by one space. Person music credits collapse tracks into one album card and show roles/year without track counts. Role and lane filters render only when more than one real option exists. Utility actions use larger icon-left-of-label controls on a left-growing row beneath the primary action. Jump to appears beside the array heading only above ten entries; beside a Series heading on wider screens it is as wide as its longest entry, so entries do not wrap; a movie Series is one full-width rail of every movie (other runs page six at a time); every full-width row below the hero (the Series, episode or owned-works row, Also in this world, More by) is headed by the one shared collapse arrow (`AppCollapsibleRow`): collapsed it is a slim bar with its title and count, the person's open or closed choice is remembered per kind of row in that browser, and a movie's Series row starts collapsed only when it has nothing else to show (no other owned movie, no missing movie for Show missing, no other Series to switch to); track lists keep their scrollbar space, so Show missing never shifts the columns; alternate series/arc selectors align left and Show missing aligns right. Person works use managed portrait covers/posters or square album art, never cinematic landscape backgrounds, and prefer owned-asset canonical titles over parent collection labels. Overview combines attributed description or biography, a purpose-built cast/credits peek, and applicable series or collection context; Detail tabs per type: movie, TV show and season **Overview, Cast, Details**; episode **Overview, Cast, Details**; album **Overview, Details**; audiobook **Overview, Tracks, Details**; book and comic **Overview, Credits, Details**; series types and Collection **Overview, Details**; a Universe page **Overview** (with the owned items in it) and **Details**; a person one page with no tabs. Cast shows each actor with the role name they played, and links to the person. There is no Character page, no Characters, Portrayals or Relationships tab and no View Appearances button; character and place links across formats belong to the Universe plan. No detail page has a Universe or Related tab, on any device; the Universe explorer and Collections stay reachable from their own places, and a stale `?tab=universe` or `?tab=related` link falls back to the first tab. **Details** reads top to bottom: file facts, then History. File facts are whatever the data store already holds per item (file name and format, container, length, pages, video, audio and subtitle summary, language, and per file the size such as `1.4 GB` or `312 MB`, the date it was added such as `2026-10-16`, the bitrate such as `6.2 Mbps` or `256 kbps`, and the audio and subtitle tracks when more than the summary already says); everything comes from stored rows and nothing is probed on page open, so a fact that is not stored is simply left out, shown on every device as a compact two-column list that wraps on phones, with one block per file when an item has several; groupings (shows, seasons, albums, series, collections) own no file and point to the items inside. When an AI feature plugin (Vibe tags, Description insights) has left anything for the item, a **Suggested by Local AI** panel marked AI follows the Details list with its short summary, vibes, themes, moods and content warnings; with the plugins off nothing AI shows anywhere. Library browse pages add **Vibes** and **Themes** filters beside Genre, listed only when at least one item has any, and carried in the page address as `vibe` and `theme`. The file path and History appear only to people who are already offered the Edit action, and History is a read-only list shown only on desktop-class screens, read from `GET /library/items/{id}/history`. Standard collection details use the same resolved membership as the Collections catalog. Listen playlists remain specialized lane-local surfaces. On phones the Listen song lists (All songs, album and artist songs, playlists) and the album details track list become card lists: each song shows its title, then artist and album, its length on the right, a 44px Favourite heart and a row menu (a bottom sheet) with Play next, Add to queue, Go to album and Go to artist; a playlist adds Move up, Move down (disabled at the ends) and a destructive Remove. Tapping a card plays it, and long song lists load 120 songs at a time behind a *Show more songs* button. Nothing on touch depends on drag or hover: drag handles and column resize grips appear only with a mouse at the desktop width, a playlist's rows reorder from the row menu on touch and with Alt+Up / Alt+Down on a focused row on desktop and TV (the move is announced), and the row menu button is always visible on touch. Below 1024px the now-playing bar's title block and transport share the width instead of holding fixed widths. On phones (the phone form factor) the hero is sized to its content under the top bar instead of filling the screen: the primary action is full width and 48px high, My List, Rate and More are 44px round icons with their names underneath, fact rows wrap as chips, genre chips keep a 44px tap box, and More opens a bottom sheet listing the same actions. The section tabs keep a 112px minimum width, fade at the edge that still has tabs and scroll the active tab into view; the episode and works rails swipe with the next card peeking, credits and people stay in one or two columns, and track rows fit the screen without sideways scrolling. Tablet and desktop keep the composition above.

A detail page never shows a button that goes nowhere: the main button exists only when there is something to play or read (Watch, Resume, Read, Listen, Play album, Explore Timeline), so a page with nothing playable, such as a track with no readable or listenable format, a show with no owned episode or a universe without a timeline, has no main button and no generic "Open" fallback. Every action the Engine sends either carries a route to open or is one the Dashboard handles itself (`DetailActionKeys`), and a test fails when one does neither. A detail page that cannot open says why in one of three ways: "not found" (also used for items outside the active profile's access), "not allowed", or "Engine unreachable" with a Retry button; only the connection failure offers Retry.

## Picture sizes (right-sized pictures, October 2026)

Every picture loads at about the size it is drawn, never the downloaded original, except the artwork picker's full view and downloads. The rule: ask for the smallest copy (tiny 96, small 320, medium 960, large 2160 px on the long side) that is at least the drawn size times the screen's sharpness. Fixed-size pictures offer the next copy up to sharp screens; screen-wide pictures let the browser choose medium on phones and large on desktop and TV. A picture seen once is kept by the browser and not fetched again until it changes; an edited picture gets a new address and shows straight away. Photos in View keep their own sizes (160-2048).

| Surface | Drawn (CSS px, long side) | Copy asked for |
|---|---|---|
| People list, Universe cast lists, family tree, Events/Added rows, table and song-row covers, Continue strip | 32-64 | tiny (96); small on sharp screens |
| Now playing bar, queue and history rows, editor thumbnails | 48-128 | tiny or small by drawn size; next copy up on sharp screens |
| Credit card faces and character pictures | up to 140 | small |
| Home, Watch, Listen, Read tiles and shelves | 128-342 | small; medium on sharp screens (browser choice) |
| Search results, Listen album/artist art, Series and album rails | 160-400 | small or medium by drawn size |
| Tile hover panel, details cover, person portrait, full player, editor artwork preview | 320-640 | medium |
| Hero carousel, details and phone hero backgrounds | full width | medium on phones, large on desktop and TV (browser choice) |
| Artwork picker "full view" and downloads | full | original |

Logos have no copies and load as stored. A new screen that draws an Engine picture without a size fails the build (`PictureSizeGuardrailTests`).

## Shelves and View all grids (October 2026)

Approved by the product owner on 2026-10-16 and 2026-10-20.

**Shelves.** Every Home and lane shelf of titles shows up to 20 items (author and genre shelves too, still at most three of each). Every shelf with a See all link ends with a **View all** tile as tall as the row's tiles (arrow and the words View all, a 48 px minimum target, reachable with a keyboard or TV remote, named "View all {shelf}"); the header link stays (See All on phones, View all elsewhere). The tile and the link go to the same place: the shelf's View all grid, or, for shelves whose own page already lists everything, that page (Series, Artists, Playlists, Favorite Songs, audiobook series, Collections & Lists, and the Collections and For Me shelves).

**Which shelves have a grid.** Home Continue Across Media, Watch, Read and Listen; Read's Continue reading, Recently added and author shelves; Watch's Continue watching, TV Shows, Movies and genre shelves; Listen's Continue listening, Recently played, recently updated albums, recently added audiobooks, New tracks added and Albums (the music shelves all open the albums grid in their own order); and My Library's My List and Liked. Each grid is at `/all/{lane}/{shelf}` and shows the shelf's whole list, not just the first 20.

**The grid.** The shelf's title as the heading and a count ("312 titles"), then sort and filters, then tiles that load more as the person scrolls (a **Load more** button stays under the grid for keyboards, remotes and screen readers). TV episodes appear as one show card and tracks as one album card, exactly as on the shelf.

**Sorts.** Recently added (the default), Title A–Z, Year (newest first, unknown years last) and Recently played (most recent first; never-played titles last, newest first). Continue and Recently played grids open on Recently played. On My List and Liked, Recently added means when the title was saved or liked. The sort chosen on a grid is remembered for the profile, on every device, per lane: Watch, Read, Listen, My List and Liked each keep their own. Continue and Recently played grids always open in their own order; re-sorting them lasts for that visit only. If the sort can't be saved it still applies for that visit and a small notice says so.

**Filters.** Only the filters that mean something for that list are shown, and a filter with fewer than two choices is hidden: **Media type** (on mixed lists such as Home's Watch, Read and Listen, My List and Liked; My List and Liked also offer Collections), **Genre** (not on a genre shelf's grid), **Format** (EPUB, PDF and comic files for reading; 4K, HD and SD for watching; never for Listen) and **Progress** (Not started, In progress, Finished; never on Continue or Recently played grids, and not while the list includes music, which has no progress). A title matches a format when any copy the person may see has it; a show's progress comes from its episodes. Each choice shows its count. On phones the sort and filters sit in the shared **Filters** bottom sheet ("Show N titles"); elsewhere they sit above the grid. Chosen filters show as removable chips with Clear all. Sort and filters are kept in the page address (replacing the history entry, so they add no Back steps), so reload and Back return to the same view.

**States.** The first load holds a skeleton grid the size of the tiles. A list that a filter empties says "Nothing matches these filters" with Clear filters; an empty list says "Nothing here yet". A grid that doesn't exist says "This list isn't available" with a link Home. A failed page keeps what is loaded and offers Try again.

**Who sees what.** Grids are read from the same authorized projection as lane pages: the profile's content limit, its lane (area) access and the libraries hidden from the profile apply, Documents never appear, a format from a copy in a library the account can't see is never offered or counted, and My List and Liked only ever show the signed-in profile's own lists (another profile's private collection or playlist never appears even if its id was saved). The TV app's "See all" reads the same full list.

## Home page: top banner, pick-up row, filters and hover (October 2026)

Approved by the product owner on 2026-10-27 (Home page redesign spec, decisions D1 to D6).

**Top banner (the hero).** It holds at most five slides. Everything the person is in the middle of comes first, most recently touched first, then newly added items, newest first. A show is one slide: a show with an episode started shows that episode (Resume, or Watch Next Episode when the last one is finished) with the show's logo; a show never started shows the series with "Start watching". Book covers in the banner are the same size as on the item's own page. Only items that have finished matching and have real artwork appear. The banner is built in two steps: the person's settings decide which items are allowed, then an orderer puts only those in order. The default orderer is the order above; a plugin's suggestion can lift a new item inside the "new" group but never ahead of something in progress. If a smarter orderer is added later and is off or fails, the default order applies.

**Banner settings (Settings, Preferences, Home).** Each profile can turn the banner off (Home then starts at its first row), choose one to five slides, allow or block Watch, Read and Listen items, and choose "continued plus new" or "continued only". They only narrow what the banner picks from. The Engine applies them, so phones, TVs and browsers agree.

**Plugin slides.** A plugin may suggest an item for the banner with a short reason. The slide is drawn by the Library, labelled "Suggested by {plugin}" (plus "AI" when Local AI produced it) with the reason on hover, and only appears for an item that already passes every check above. Text with markup, script or an address, and icons that are not a plain icon name, are refused and the reason is logged. Each profile can switch a plugin's slides off.

**Pick up where you left off.** One horizontal row of cards, one card per thing started, whatever its type (a show once, an album as "Recently played"). Each card has the cover, the type with its icon, the title, a subtitle, a progress bar with a line such as "42% watched", and a round play button. The title opens the item's page. With nothing started the row is not shown.

**Recently Added filters.** The pills are toggles: All, Watch, Read, Listen, View. A type with nothing in the library has no pill, and two or more can be on at once. The tiles are 25 percent smaller than shelf tiles.

**One hover.** Movie and TV tiles on Home and Watch share one hover: after a short pause the tile lifts a little and the preview fades in over it. The tile never changes width, so neighbours do not move. Pause, fade and hide use one set of timings, and moving straight from one tile to the next skips the pause. Nothing happens on touch screens or with reduced motion on.

**Opening an item.** Clicking a tile hands its cover, logo and backdrop to the item's page, so the page shows the real picture and title straight away instead of grey boxes while the rest loads. A link typed or reloaded keeps the grey stand-ins.

## When an item appears: Ready

Every library surface (Home, the hero, the shelves and lanes, Watch, Read, Listen, collections, People pages and the Library list) shows an item only when it is **Ready**. An item is Ready when it has been matched to a real title, is placed in its show or album (an episode under its show, a track under its album; books and comics that belong to no series stand alone), has its artwork (found, or searched for and settled), is not waiting in Needs Review, and has not been rejected. Wikidata and the later enrichment steps are not required, so items appear quickly and fill in by themselves while you already watch or read.

Ready is decided once by the Engine and is the only gate; no screen has its own rule. It is sticky: once an item is Ready, matching it again (a rematch, a refresh) does not make it disappear. It leaves only on a negative outcome: it lands in Needs Review, finds no match or fails, is rejected, loses its file, or loses its place in its show or album. When it is Ready again it returns without being announced a second time.

An item that is not Ready appears only on the live ingestion page (Settings, Library and Ingestion), which says what each one is still waiting for. Each item appears on its own as soon as it is Ready; nothing waits for the rest of a batch. "Added" means Ready: the "added" notice and the Recently Added entry happen at that moment, once per item.

## Collections presentation

Current presentation rules: the Collections landing route is labeled **Discovery**, while Automatic, Curated, Shelves, and People retain their names. Shelves opens with Read, Watch, and Listen preview lanes and filters by Books, Movies, and Audiobooks. Albums and comic issue series remain native lane groups. Same-name duplicate contributor records collapse into one enriched shelf presentation. Discover rows show a small title and optional year for individual items, but group tiles do not repeat text they already embed. TV Shows rows keep episode counts but omit the redundant `TV Show` pill. Completed TV shows show a provider-backed premiere-to-finale year range; TV detail pages always keep the season selector visible, including when only one season is owned.

A curated collection (a manually built Custom collection) can never be empty. The editor does not let it be created or saved without at least one item and sends the chosen items with the create request; the Engine refuses to take out its last item or its last attached personal-media source ("A collection needs at least one item. Delete the collection instead.") and refuses an update that would leave it empty. When the last item of a curated collection leaves the library the collection is deleted automatically and a "Removed the collection 'X' because its last item left the library." entry is written to the activity history. Playlists, playlist folders and rule-driven (live) collections are not affected, and automatic collections follow the canonical metadata. Deleting a title from the library names each hand-picked collection it sits in, and a hand-picked collection left with nothing in it is deleted with the title. Automatic collections are never named (see Delete from library).

## Delete from library

Delete from library is the one way to remove a title from the library. It is for administrators on a desktop with a fine, hover-capable pointer (`EditorDeviceRule.CanEdit`), and the Engine checks every call. It appears in three places and nowhere else:

- **Detail page.** **Delete from library…** is the last entry in the page's **...** menu, in red below a divider, whether or not Editing Mode is on. When Editing Mode is on it is also in the editing bar's **...** menu. Both open the same dialog. It is offered only for single titles such as a movie, an episode, a book, an audiobook or a comic issue.

If someone else removes a title while you have it open, the page stays open with a notice that the title is no longer in the library, and Editing Mode ends. Anything you had typed in a field is kept on the notice with a **Copy** button, so it is not lost.
- **Listen song table.** Each song row has a Delete icon, and selecting songs shows "N selected", **Delete…** and **Clear**. Phones, touch-only devices and anyone who is not an administrator see neither.
- **Settings > Libraries > Excluded files.** The files that stayed on disk, each with **Add back**.

The dialog first checks what will happen ("Checking what will be deleted…") and then shows:

- **Files**: every media file and sidecar, with its folder and size, each marked as deleted from disk or as staying where it is (in a read-only folder, the original of a merge, or a file that is protected or cannot be removed). Files that stay are excluded from future scans.
- **Hand-picked collections**: each collection the title is in that someone made by hand, with a note that the title is taken out of it. A hand-picked collection left with nothing in it is deleted too, and the dialog names it. Automatic collections are never listed.
- **Progress**: how many people have progress on the title. The count is shown, not their names, and the progress is removed.
- **Space freed**, and "Files deleted from disk can't be recovered." when at least one file will be deleted.

The red button reads **Delete from library and disk** when at least one file will be deleted, and **Delete from library** when every file stays. Cancel has the focus when the dialog opens, and Escape cancels. Collections are sorted A to Z, and after five the list ends with "and N more". The dialog warns when processing for the title will be stopped. If the plan has changed since the dialog opened, the dialog shows the updated list and nothing is deleted.

Library media has no recycle bin. Films, TV, books, audiobooks, comics and music can be downloaded again, so deletion is immediate after the confirmation. Only titles and songs can be deleted this way. A whole show, season, album or series, a single edition or file, and personal items in View cannot be deleted this way yet.

## Universe page

A Universe has its own page, `/universe/{qid}`: a page to read, not a management screen. It is not a menu item and the phone dock does not change.

**Finding it.** Collections lists Universes in the **Universes** group; a Universe tile opens the page (a Universe without a Wikidata identity keeps the ordinary collection page). Search results for a Universe open it. The Universe details route (`/details/universe/{id}`) has one main button, **Open Universe**. An item page shows one quiet line under its title block, "Part of the Dune Universe" (a name that already ends in "Universe" is not given a second one, and one that starts with "The" does not get a second "the": "Part of the Expanse Universe"), linking to the page; it appears only when the Library holds two or more Series, or two or more formats, in that Universe that the profile can see. It is never a tab and never a button row.

**Sections, in order, each hidden when empty:**
1. **Hero**: the Universe's name, a stack of covers from its first Series, and one line of totals: "3 Series · 14 Works · 2 formats" (singular and plural are respected; a Universe made only of TV shows says "shows"; standalone works count toward the Work and format totals, and a Universe whose only visible works are standalone still opens). A small "Enrichment running" indicator, blue like the menu indicator, shows while the Engine is still learning about its works.
2. **Series**: one shelf per Series (or show) in the order the Engine gives it, numbered as the source gives it (decimals kept). "Also in this world" sits under its Series. Entries the Library does not own appear as dashed "Not in library" placeholders in their place only when the profile chose to see missing entries for that Series (its own choice, otherwise the media default); otherwise they are not drawn and the Series shows a quiet "N missing" count.
3. **Standalone**: works that belong to the Universe but to no Series, as one shelf.
4. **Connections**, evidence only, every row naming where it came from ("Source: Wikidata", or the approved lore source's name such as "Sources: Wikidata, Dune Wiki"): Cast (headshot, name, "as Character, Character"; a row opens **Also appears in**: every owned Work that shares one of that person's characters, then more of the person's own Work in the Library, each linking onward; the person's page is one link away from there), Adaptations ("Title — Based on Source", only real based-on, derivative or inspired-by links) and Characters (a read-only list: name, "In 3 of your works" and its source, the characters seen in the most works first; a row opens **Also appears in** for that character; the first 12 show with a "Show all" toggle that stays in place as "Show fewer", at most 60 are listed with "Showing the 60 characters seen in the most works" when there are more; no portraits, no relationship graph, no character page). Spoiler respect, one rule for every place a character is named (Characters, Cast, **Also appears in**, the explorer graph): with your spoiler level on **Up to where I am** (the default) a character appears only once you have reached them. A character first seen in an episode you own that is later than the furthest episode you have started is left out, and one honest line under the list says "3 characters hidden to avoid spoilers"; a Cast row keeps the person and drops only those characters' names (so the row has no "as ..." when none remain); **Also appears in** for such a character says it is hidden to avoid spoilers and lists nothing. A character the Library cannot place within what you have watched or read, because it appears only in a show, film or book you have not started, or somewhere within a title with no episode to place it by (books and audiobooks never have chapter data), is listed last with its name, source and a quiet "No plot details" tag only: no work count and no description. It still opens **Also appears in**, which lists Works rather than plot. A character you have met in any one title it appears in (a film you finished, an episode you have reached) is shown, even when another title first sees them later. A character marked as a spoiler for a work you have not finished (or do not own) is hidden. With the level on **Everything** nothing is held back. You choose the level yourself in Settings, Preferences, Playback, **Spoilers** (Up to where I am / Everything); nobody else, not even a household administrator, can change it for you. The same rule covers every other place a character is named (Shy's decision, 2026-10-20): the "as Character" lines on a title's cast list and on a person's page, and the people search, which neither matches nor names ("Plays ...") a character you have not reached. The actor always still shows, just without the hidden character names. A title you have finished shows everyone in it, even a character the Library cannot place at an episode. The admin-only Universe editor is a correction tool and is not filtered. Lists sit side by side as room allows on tablet and desktop, stacked on a phone. Desktop only, an **Open the explorer** link goes to `/universe/{qid}/explore` for paths and the family tree; the explorer's adaptation tree likewise lists only real adaptation links, each with its source, and says plainly when there are none. With nothing recorded and enrichment not finished it says "Still learning about this Universe" with a quiet note such as "Details found for 6 of 14 works"; with nothing recorded and enrichment finished it says "No characters, cast or adaptations recorded for this Universe yet." It never shows sample data. For administrators, when the Fandom lore plugin is off, a quiet "Add Fandom details" link goes to Settings, Plugins.

**States.** Loading shows skeletons for the hero and Series. If the Engine cannot be reached the page says "Can't reach the Engine" with Retry. A Universe the profile cannot see (or that does not exist) says "This Universe isn't available." The page re-reads itself at most once every ten seconds while enrichment events arrive.

**Editing.** Desktop administrators who may edit the Universe see **Edit** in the hero. It asks for the administrator unlock, then opens the Universe editor in the same side drawer Editing Mode uses, beside the page, not as a modal; saving a change refreshes the page. Phones, tablets, TVs and everyone else see no Edit control. TV: cards and links are focusable in order with a visible focus ring and nothing is hover-only. Phone: no sideways page scroll, 16 px gutters, 44 px targets.

**Helper apps.** The Android, Apple and TV web apps show the same hero line, Series (in the same order) with "Also in this world" beneath each, and read-only Cast and Adaptations lists from `GET /api/v1/universe/{qid}`, with no graph, Standalone shelf, missing placeholders or editing; Roku lists the Universe's Series only. Details: [Native clients](native-clients.md#collections-and-universes-in-apps).

## Phone companion

The companion is a read-only screen for following a story on a phone or tablet while something plays elsewhere, or while you read, at `/companion` and `/companion/{workId}`. It is offered on phones and tablets only: on a desktop or TV the title menu item and the Home card are hidden and the page says it is for phones and tablets (`CompanionDeviceRule`). It opens with one plain sentence saying what it is, with an (i) tooltip on how to use it. It is not a menu item and the phone dock does not change. It never edits anything; Editing Mode stays desktop-only.

**Getting there.** Home shows a **Now playing** card above the shelves only while the active profile is playing or has paused something on one of its devices ("Lanterns · S1E2, on Living Room TV") with a **Companion** button; with one device the button follows it, with several the card says "and 1 more" and the button opens the companion to choose. Nothing playing means no card. Every title's "..." menu (film, episode, book, audiobook, comic issue) has a **Companion** item that opens `/companion/{workId}`: it holds that title and opens the episode or chapter list so you can pick where you are. On a phone the "..." menu is the bottom sheet. Only the profile's own devices are ever listed; what someone else in the house is playing is never shown.

**The screen**, one column on a phone and 44 px targets throughout: the title and your place ("Season 1 · Episode 2", "Chapter 12: The Gate", "Film"), whether you are following a device ("Following Living Room TV") or holding a place you set; a small **Up to here / Everything** switch (the profile's own Spoilers preference, the same one as Settings, Preferences, Playback, so changing it in either place changes both); buttons to open the item page and its Universe; the synopsis; **Who's here** (characters up to your place, with source and performer, judged by the same spoiler rule as the Universe page; a character the Library cannot place is named with "No plot details"; characters held back are counted in one honest line); **Where you've seen them** (characters that also appear in other Works you own; a row opens **Also appears in**); **Previously** (the last episode you own before this one, with its synopsis); and a **Change episode / Change chapter / Set my place** button that opens the picker. Following refreshes about every five seconds; a place you set never changes by itself. When the followed device stops, the screen keeps where you were and says "Playback stopped."

**Honest limits.** A Work with no character details says "No character details for this one yet." (with the cast and synopsis it does have); a Work whose details exist but are all ahead of you says "Characters show up here as you reach them."; a missing synopsis says "No synopsis for this one yet."; books and audiobooks have no chapter-level source, so their characters show without plot details, and the screen says why. A book without a table of contents gets a chapter-number stepper instead of a list. A film is held as a whole (no scene data), and characters in a film you are watching are named without plot details until you have finished it. Nothing is ever invented to fill a gap. Characters never appear on item pages (they live on the Universe page, the companion and Also appears in).

## Series rails and app shell

Canonical book, comic, and movie series containers show their sequence rail directly on Overview. Source numbering stays above each cover (decimal positions such as 1.5 are shown as the source gives them; a member with no position shows a dot, not a made-up number), connectors appear behind number nodes only between members that sit next to each other in the Engine's order, and the current item uses a stronger purple frame glow without `This book`, `This movie`, or `Up next` labels. Only main-sequence members are numbered and counted in "x of y". Supplementary, collected-content and broader-context members of the same Series appear in a separate **Also in this world** row under the rail, on the Series page and on the item page, and are never numbered with the run. A book Series, an audiobook Series and a TV show that share a name are separate Series. Completion remains a separate check state, and `aria-current` preserves accessible current-item context. Missing-item visibility inherits its media default from `config/ui/library-preferences.json`; the database stores only explicit profile-and-series overrides, which can be removed to restore config inheritance.

A standalone work (no collection, no series value and no parent) never shows a series or sequence panel on its details page. Opening any details page from a browse surface starts at the top, and Back restores the previous page's scroll position.

`MainLayout` exposes My List as the active profile's saved shortlist and delegates account actions to `TopNavAccountMenu`. Needs Review lives inside that permission-aware menu rather than in a standalone bell. `SystemActivityIndicator` uses `ShellActivityState` to combine ingestion (the Engine's single queue snapshot), AI download/parsing, enrichment and durable-operation activity into one circular progress surface with exactly two states: blue (`--tl-accent-secondary`) while the Library is working and green (`--tl-status-success`) when it is idle, with no amber or other state. Playback does not light it (it is not Library work), and a queue that is only waiting for a missing tool keeps the indicator green, though missing tools and Needs Review items also appear in the administrator banner. It shows blue only while the Engine's snapshot is under 15 seconds old and the live connection is up. The tooltip uses the live page's words ("Found 508 files · 120 of 508 done · About 4 min left", "Found 508 files · 120 of 508 done · Running for 4 min" when the Engine has no reliable estimate, "Waiting for media tools · 3 files are waiting" while tools are missing, or "Library is up to date · Last activity 3 minutes ago" when idle), the state is also written as text for assistive technology, and a click opens the live Ingestion page. The account menu offers **My profile** (the person's Overview) and **My activity** (the Activity page under it), shows each person's avatar beside their name in the profile switcher, offers Manage profiles to administrators, and offers Sign out for the current session, including local accounts. Below 600 px (the phone form factor) navigation is the three-tab floating dock, the hamburger menu and the section chips described under *Phone navigation* below. Tablets and desktops show the top navigation and no dock. At 840 px and narrower (portrait tablets, narrow desktop windows) the top bar stays the same but compact: the icon logo instead of the wordmark, the account button as the avatar alone, and the section labels scroll sideways in their own space if they still do not fit, so they never sit under Search, My List or library activity. TV uses the five-tab dock (Home, Libraries, Search, Downloads, Settings) from `shell.intent_dock_items`, where **Libraries** goes to the library surface the profile used last (Watch until one has been used; remembered per profile in browser storage under `tuvima.last-library.v1.<profile id>`), is active on Watch, Read, Listen, View, Collections and Recently added, and, tapped while already on one of those surfaces, opens the library switcher (a bottom sheet of six 56 px rows: Watch, Read, Listen, View, Collections, Recently added). **Search** opens the full-screen search overlay (on phones safe-area aware, with a visible Cancel button, the on-screen keyboard's search action, and results as a one-column list of tappable rows). **Downloads** opens `/downloads`, which says downloads live in the Tuvima app; `ShellDock.DownloadsMode` switches between that page and dropping the tab. The older names Read, Watch, Listen, View and Collections stay valid in the TV list so saved settings still resolve. A Kids profile (restricted role) gets the simpler Kids shell: the top bar keeps only Watch, Listen and Read and My List, the phone dock is the same three tabs as everyone's (the phone menu and chips list only Home, Watch, Listen and Read, with no Settings or Recently added; the TV switcher lists only Watch, Listen and Read and has no Settings tab), tiles are larger with rounder corners (`.layout-kids` tokens in `wwwroot/app.css`), the account menu drops My profile, Settings and Needs Review, keeps **My activity** (which opens the Kids page `/my-activity` rather than Settings) and shows a *Kids* badge. Content limits are separate and not part of the shell.

My List uses a heart everywhere: the top-bar My List button, the one My List heart in the top bar (the Read, Watch and Listen side menus carry no My List row of their own; Listen keeps Favorites under Music / Playlists), the Home hero and phone hero card, detail pages, saved Playlists, My Library and activity rows. The heart is outlined when the item is not saved and filled once it is (a toggle labelled Add to My List, then In My List); a menu or top-bar entry that only opens My List keeps its surface's usual icon style. This supersedes the list-with-tick, "+"/"✓" and ringed-planet glyphs in older notes below. A song's Favorite heart in Listen is unchanged: it still adds the song to Favorites, which is separate from My List. The bookmark shape is reserved for real bookmarks. The account menu header shows the account email. The profile switcher appears only with more than one profile, and the menu includes Manage profiles (administrators) and, for this-computer-only accounts, a light red **Secure your account** card at the very top of the menu above the name ("Add a password to use the Library away from home. Phones and TVs at home keep working without one." with **Secure account** and **Not now**; there is no avatar dot). Not now is remembered with the account, so it follows the person to any browser. There is no bar across the top of the screen for this-computer-only accounts: the prompt lives in the account menu card, Settings > Security (see below) and the "Add a password to your account first" toast.

## Phone navigation (under 600 px)

> **Reversal.** This replaces the five-tab phone dock (Home, Libraries, Search, Downloads, Settings, with no hamburger and the Libraries switcher sheet) that earlier revisions of this page described. The Libraries tab, the Downloads tab and the Settings tab are gone from the phone dock; the library switcher and the five-tab dock remain on TV only. Tablet, desktop and TV navigation are unchanged.

The phone shell has four parts, all on the phone form factor only (`html[data-form-factor="phone"]`):

- **Top bar.** A fixed 56 px bar row: a 48 px **hamburger** on the left, the icon-only logo (`tuvima-icon.svg`, no wordmark), then on the right the Downloads icon (see below), My List, library activity and the account avatar. There is no search icon: Search is a dock tab, so there is one Search. The bar floats over the Home and details heroes with the same translucent gradient and blur as before, and is a solid surface on other pages.
- **Section chips.** A fixed 48 px row under the bar (so the whole top chrome, `--app-topbar-height`, is 104 px): **Read, Watch, Listen, View, Collections**, in the order the desktop top bar uses, scrolling sideways with a fade at the edge that still has chips. The chip for the section the person is in is selected (`aria-current="page"`, accent border and soft fill, and it scrolls into view). The row has one tab stop and the left and right arrow keys, Home and End move between chips. Held sideways (under 480 px tall) there is no room, so the row is dropped and the menu still lists every section.
- **Floating dock.** A rounded pill of three 48 px tabs, the same for everyone including Kids: **Home**, **Search** (opens the full-screen search overlay) and **My Library**. It sits clear of the screen edges and the bottom safe area, has its own grid row so it never covers the last row of a page, and sits below the audio player. **My Library** opens the My Library page (`/my-library`, described below) and is selected there and on the personal grids behind its See All links (`/for-me`); the details-page **+ My List** action keeps its name.
- **Menu.** The hamburger opens a full-screen sheet (a native modal dialog: focus stays inside, Escape closes it, the browser's Back also closes it and returns to the page, and focus goes back to the hamburger; it only animates when the person has not asked for reduced motion; every row is 56 px tall and labelled). It is generated from the same catalogue as the desktop top bar (`ShellNavigation`, so it cannot drift): **Home**, **My Library** (the dock tab's page), **Recently added**, then the top bar lanes in the top bar's order (Read, Watch, Listen, View, Collections), each with its icon. Photos and Documents are not separate entries: they live inside View. Below a divider: **Settings**, **Switch profile** (only when the household has more than one profile) and **Sign out**. Every entry appears only if the active profile may see it, using the same rules as the desktop bar and account menu, so a Kids profile sees Home, My Library, Watch, Listen and Read and Sign out only, and a profile that may not see Documents never meets the word in the menu, the chips or the dock.

The **Downloads** icon sits on the right of the top bar only where downloads actually work, which is the Tuvima app. The browser Dashboard cannot download, so `ShellDock.DownloadsMode` ships as `ExplainNativeApp` and the icon is hidden there; `DownloadsTabMode.Available` shows it.

On Home the phone hero card is sized from the chrome above and the dock below (`--phone-hero-bottom-bar` in `PhoneHeroCard.razor.css` is the one number that sets it), so adding the chips row did not move the first shelf header; the card is correspondingly shorter than when the bar was 65 px with no chips.

## My Library page

My Library is the personal page, named like My Netflix. It combines Continue, My List and the other personal sections in one responsive column that phones show first; the desktop and tablet top bar is unchanged (its My List button lands on the same page). Route `/my-library`; the heading and the browser tab title say My Library. Old `/for-me?view=my-list` links land on it. See All on **My List** opens the My List grid (`/all/personal/my-list`) and on **Liked** the Liked grid (`/all/personal/liked`), the same View all grid every shelf uses (see *Shelves and View all grids*). Old `/for-me?view=my-list&area=all` and `/for-me?view=liked` links land on those grids; an older link that carries a lane area (`area=read|watch|listen`) still opens the filtered `/for-me` grid.

Top to bottom, each section hidden when it has nothing to show:

1. **Profile header.** Avatar, My Library and the profile name, a **Switch profile** button (only when the household has more than one profile) and a **Settings** link (never for Kids). No account editing.
2. **Downloads card.** A link to `/downloads`, shown only where downloads work (`ShellDock.ShowsDownloadsInTopBar`, that is `DownloadsTabMode.Available`, the Tuvima app). It is not rendered at all in the browser.
3. **Continue.** Every media type in one row, using `PhoneContinueLogic` and the phone Continue row (tablets and desktops keep the per-type groups). Source: `GET /display/continue`.
4. **My List.** Saved items, newest first (at most 20 on the shelf), with See All. Never hidden: when empty it keeps its title and says "Add titles with the My List heart", with no buttons. Source: the saved ids resolved through the Engine's direct item lookup (`POST /display/items`, see *Personal pages resolve ids directly* below).
5. **Recent activity.** A short preview of the Activity page (the latest six entries, music collapsed into one session tile), with **See all** opening the full list: `/settings/overview/activity`, or `/my-activity` for a Kids profile, which has no Settings. It replaces the old History shelf, so there is one list of what the profile did. Hidden when there is nothing yet. Source: `GET /api/v1/activity/recent?limit=6`, drawn with the Overview's `RecentActivityStrip`.
6. **Liked.** Everything marked Like or Love, newest first (at most 20 on the shelf), with See All. Source: profile reactions resolved through the same item lookup.

Kids profiles get the same sections, narrowed by the Engine's existing permission and content rules (every read is profile-scoped). A profile that may not see Documents never meets a Document item (they are hidden until the View settings say Documents are allowed). There are no editing controls on the page.

**Loading.** The header loads first, then every section loads in parallel. Until the header is in, only the heading and a header skeleton show. Each section holds a fixed-height skeleton (Continue about 285 px and every shelf 341 px at 390 px wide, the same height as the loaded row) and sections reveal top to bottom, so a section that turns out empty can only move skeletons below it, never content that is on screen. Artwork is the sized renditions only. A section whose read fails is left out and one quiet notice with Try again appears after the last section. On phones every shelf header, including this page's, is one 48 px row with the title on the left and See All on the right.

**Personal pages resolve ids directly.** My Library and For Me turn the saved, liked and favorite ids into tiles with `POST /api/v1/display/items` (`PersonalTileIndex.LookupAsync`), not by reading the Read, Watch and Listen lane browse pages, so an item is never missing just because it sits past a lane page's size. The Dashboard sends at most 200 ids per call (the Engine's cap) and splits a longer list into several calls, one page back. The Engine answers from the same authorized projection as the lane pages, so the profile's content limit and the profile's lanes and hidden libraries apply exactly as they do on a lane page; an id the profile cannot see, a Collection or Playlist id (those come from the Collection catalogue and Playlist reads) and an id that does not exist all simply have no card. Documents are never returned, so a profile that may not see them cannot learn that one exists, and the Dashboard still hides any Document tile it is handed. Collections and Playlists keep their own reads. If the lookup or the recent-activity read fails, that section is left out and the quiet notice appears, rather than the row showing as empty.

## TV form factor, focus and spatial navigation

The tv form factor (`html[data-form-factor="tv"]`, no layout redesign) is a larger, remote-friendly presentation of the same pages. One block in `wwwroot/app.css` sets an 18px root font size, 56px dock targets with 14px labels, one step larger tile titles, a 48px shelf gutter and a 60ch maximum line length for hero subtitles and synopses. Phone and desktop layouts are unchanged. Every focusable control (tiles, dock items, chips, buttons, table rows, hero controls) shows one shared focus ring on keyboard or remote focus: the `--tl-focus-ring` colour with `--tl-focus-ring-width` and `--tl-focus-ring-offset`; text fields keep their own field focus styling. Spatial navigation (`registerSpatialNav` in `wwwroot/app.js`) moves focus with the arrow keys by on-screen position: left and right stay within a shelf, up and down move between shelves, the dock and the hero. Enter activates the focused control, Escape or Backspace go back, and the focused tile scrolls into view. It is always on in the tv form factor; on other form factors it starts after the first arrow key pressed while focus is on a shelf tile. It never acts inside text fields, selects, sliders or editable regions, or while a dialog is open.

## TV episode routing and heroes

TV episodes are children of the show detail page, organized by season. A combined file (one file, several episodes) is named with its episode run when the organiser renames files, for example `s01e01-e02`; every episode it covers is shown as owned. Each owned
episode has a show-scoped detail route opened from its still. Continue surfaces
retain the episode still and playback target, identify it with compact copy such
as `Continue · S5 E1`, and use actions such as `Resume S5 E1`. Episode detail
heroes retain that episode's still, synopsis, and genre. Root show heroes report
owned episodes and target either the in-progress episode or the first owned episode.
An unstarted show may retain the series hero while showing that episode's facts;
after progress, the hero switches to the episode still and `Sx Ey` description.
Short provider show copy appears under the owned summary in a separated Series
Description block. Detail heroes use one left-aligned column for written identity,
logos, compact facts, actions, and description, and show at most two linked genres
on their own non-wrapping line. Movie heroes
use the movie description without a synopsis heading. The watch utility row does
not repeat a Show details action.

### Seasons open inside their show

A season has no page of its own. Opening one, from any address, shows its show's page
with that season selected in the season control and that season's episodes listed;
it never reads as "not found" while the show and at least one of the season's episodes
are visible to the profile. The address is the show's with the season added
(`/details/tvshow/{show}?season={season}`); `/details/tvseason/{season}` resolves to the
same page. The chosen season is part of the address, so reloading keeps it, and the
season control and episode list behave the same on phones, tablets and desktops. A
season whose episodes the active profile cannot see reads as not found, like any other
item the profile may not see.

### No shareable links; titles open over the current page; the tab is remembered

A library is private, so detail pages have no shareable links and no link-copy action anywhere (not in the More menu of a detail page, the Home hero or the Listen page). Links between items inside the app use the item's ID (`/details/{type}/{id}`). Target behaviour, built with the detail overlay: a title opens as an overlay over the page you were on; Back, the close button and Esc dismiss it and return to the page underneath; reloading while a title is open reopens that same title over Home so you keep your place.

The tab you choose on a detail page (and, on a show, the season) is written into the page address as `tab` and `season` by replacing the current history entry, so it never adds a Back step. Reloading, or coming back to the page, reopens that tab and season; a `tab` value that does not exist or is hidden for that title opens the default tab. Choosing a tab or season never reloads the page from the Engine, and a personal action such as marking something watched keeps the chosen tab and season.

### One file, several episodes: the editor picker

When one video file holds several episodes of a season, the editor's Matching tab
shows a **This file covers** picker for that episode's file. Each episode of the
season is a tappable chip (`E2 · Title`); the file's own episode is always ticked and
locked, episodes that already have their own file are locked, and a file covers at
most six episodes, all within one season. **Save episodes** replaces the list in one
step, answers the file's pending combined-media Review item, and records a History
entry; saving the same list again changes nothing. Single-episode files see no change
beyond the picker staying out of the way when the season has one episode. On phones
the chips stack full width with 44px touch targets and the actions stay reachable
inside the modal.

### Whole miniseries moved to TV

When a Movies item turns out to be a TMDB miniseries and **Move to TV** is used from

Another member's space is read only: no Add media, no editing, hidden photos stay hidden, and sending one of their photos to the Shared Library always makes a copy. People in other households never appear. Everyone can open and send to their household's Shared Library by default; a child profile can send only after a household administrator allows it. Household administrators (and server administrators) review contributions, and there is no curator role. A reviewer whose own Documents setting is off never learns that a document was sent: a contribution that holds only documents is not listed (and opening it by link is not found), a mixed one lists only its other items (and the log lines for them) with the single line "Includes items you cannot view" and no number, and approving, declining or retrying a contribution that holds documents is refused (the Dashboard does not offer those buttons). The same switch keeps documents out of Galleries (their items, counts and covers, including shared ones), the favourite, hide, archive and trash actions, the tag type-ahead and uploads (a document upload is refused before anything is stored).

**View > Documents** (`/view/documents`, tab beside Galleries) browses, searches and opens the documents in View. It is shown only to profiles allowed to see Documents (on for everyone except child profiles until an administrator turns it on in Settings > Libraries > View > Who can see Documents); the Engine list, open, download and text actions follow the same switch, and a profile without it sees one plain "Documents are not available for this profile" page. The scope picker matches the other View pages. Cards (grid) and rows (list) show a first-page picture for PDFs and images or a typed tile with the extension for Word, spreadsheet, slide and text files, the title, the document date (or "Added" and the day it was added), kind chips (PDF, Word, Spreadsheet, Slides, Text, Scan, Protected) the correspondent and document type as chips, and up to three tags as read-only chips. Searching waits 300 ms after typing, asks the Engine's contents index (title, file name, words, tags) for documents only and shows the matching words highlighted under the title; document words appear nowhere else: never in Home, global Search or Recommendations. Opening a PDF shows a full-window viewer (PDF.js, bundled locally) with page navigation, zoom, find (the search words are already entered) and Download original; Word, spreadsheet, slide and text files show their extracted words in a readable panel with Download original. Small notes under the title say how the words were read (Text read by OCR; password-protected so not searchable; very long so only the start is searchable; no searchable words found); a missing word layer on a photo is never shown as an error. On phones the scope and layout choices move into a bottom sheet and the list is one column; televisions can browse and open but have no Select mode.

**View on phones and touch.** Nothing in View depends on drag or hover on a touch device. The kind tabs under the Photos search (and on the Artwork Library) are one horizontal row of 44px chips that scrolls sideways, fades at the edge where more chips wait and brings the active chip into view; they never force the page wider than the screen. Photos are picked with **Select** in the page header (shown on touch devices only): tiles stop opening the viewer and become tap-to-select toggles with a checkmark and a pressed state, Space toggles the focused tile, and there is no long-press. A bar fixed above the dock (clear of the audio bar, with the side safe areas as padding) shows the count and **Add to gallery**, **Share** (the same Shared Library submission as on desktop), **Download** and **Cancel**; Favorite, Archive and Trash move into its three-dot menu, and Done or Cancel leaves Select mode and clears the selection. Touch tiles are never draggable (drag-to-gallery stays a mouse gesture) and the date heading's select-all control stays visible. Phones may add the selection to an existing Gallery but never offer New Gallery or open the Gallery editor. Artwork Library cards keep their actions (Open, Manage usage) behind a three-dot row menu: a bottom sheet on phones, the shared popover elsewhere, with a visible focus ring and the card title and facts always shown on touch. In the photo lightbox every control is 44px, the arrows stay, and a quick horizontal swipe on a fitted photo moves to the next or previous photo (a zoomed photo pans instead, a pinch zooms, and videos are not swiped). Map zoom buttons are 44px and the attribution button sits clear of the dock on phones; the geolocate control is untouched. The Documents viewer is outside these rules.

**Organising documents (tags, correspondents, types, dates, custom fields).** Tags, correspondents, document types and custom fields are one list per install, shared by photos, videos and documents (tags) and by documents (the rest). A person sees only the names used on items they can open plus ones they created and have not used yet, and the counts are their own; a name that only appears on someone else's private item is never listed, suggested or accepted by its id. Only the person who made a name, a server administrator, or a household administrator for names made in their own household can rename or delete it, and deleting never removes a document. A duplicate name in any letter case is refused with a plain message. On the Documents page a left filter rail (inside the "View options" sheet on phones) filters by tags (every chosen tag must be present), correspondent, type, a date range and choice or yes/no custom fields; each change reloads the list, counts come from the Engine, and "Clear all" resets them. On a pointer screen in the person's own space each card has an "Edit details" button and the open document has an "Edit details" action; both open one small panel over the page with title, tags, correspondent, type, date, archive number, notes and the custom fields, saved with one Save. Phones and televisions only read and filter; they never open the editor. A document's given date wins over the date in its file, then the day it was added. New documents wait in the Inbox (filing state `inbox`) and documents that existed before filing count as filed. See the Inbox paragraph below. The lists are managed in Settings > Libraries > View > Organising documents. The View shell loads the View settings once and the Documents page waits for it, so the two no longer load them twice. An empty library says "Documents added to View sources or the Import folder appear here." with the Add media action.

**Saved views, bulk editing, sorting and list export (Documents).** At the top of the Documents filter rail sit the person's **Saved views**: each is a shelf made from the filters they chose (tags where every one must be present, correspondent, type, a date range, choice or yes/no custom fields, the Inbox, and the words typed in the search box saved as "words in the document contain"). **Save this view** appears whenever the list has filters or search words and no saved view is applied (leave the applied view first: it is not copied into the new one) and the Trash is not showing; it asks for a name, then the view appears in the list. Tapping a saved view starts the list from it (tap it again to leave it); the other filters then narrow it further, so a person can open "Tax this month" and still pick one correspondent. Each row has a menu with **Rename** and **Delete**; deleting a view never touches the documents. A saved view is an ordinary Smart Gallery, so it also appears in Galleries for profiles that may use Documents; no second kind of shelf exists. It is always private: Gallery sharing refuses it (the Engine answers 403 "A saved Documents view is private and cannot be shared.") because its rule can hold search words and it lists private documents. The date range can be a fixed span or a relative one (**Last 7 days, Last 30 days, Last 90 days, This month, This year, Last year**); a relative range is saved as the range and worked out again every time the view opens, so "Last 30 days" is always the last 30 days. Saved views are offered only in the person's own space and never on televisions, and a profile that may not use Documents neither sees nor can create one (the Engine hides and refuses them, even if made before Documents was turned off). A saved view is never offered as a Collection source. The Gallery rule editor cannot show a saved view's custom field conditions, so a view with custom fields is changed by saving it again from the Documents page.

**Select mode and bulk editing (Documents).** On a computer or tablet the Documents header has **Select**; it works in all three lists (All documents, Inbox, Trash). Cards show a checkbox, a click or tap on a card toggles it, and **Shift and click** selects every document between the last one picked and the one clicked. On a phone there is no Select button: **pressing and holding a document for half a second** starts selecting (the tap that ends the hold does not open the document), further taps add or remove documents, and **Done selecting** leaves select mode. Televisions have no select mode. Picking at least one document opens the selection bar ("3 selected") with **Organise** (Add tags, Remove tags, Set correspondent, Set type, Set date, Mark filed), **Favorite**, **Trash** and a clear button; in the Trash view the bar offers **Restore** instead. Add tags, set correspondent or type and set date ask one small question first (a tag name or several, a correspondent or type name, a date, or "clear it instead") and then apply to the whole selection; a new tag, correspondent or type name is added to the global lists like anywhere else. Mark filed files documents waiting in the Inbox exactly as they are, without taking their suggestions. Moving to the trash and restoring use the existing trash lifecycle and never delete a file. One call changes at most 500 documents (a longer selection is sent in batches and reported once). Afterwards a plain notice says what happened ("Updated all 12 documents." or "Updated 9 of 12 documents. The rest were left as they were.") and lists each reason with a count, for example "2 left as they were. In the Shared Library: only filing rules can change it." or "1 left as it was. This document is not available." Every document is checked on its own: one that belongs to someone else, is outside the person's libraries, is not a document, or is in the Shared Library (only filing rules change those) is skipped with its reason and left exactly as it was, and one that fails never stops or half-changes the others. A value a person sets in bulk is recorded as the person's, so an auto-filing rule never replaces it. Removing a tag that a rule added is not remembered: the rule can add it again the next time it runs.

**Sorting the Documents list.** The filter rail has **Sort by**: Newest date first (the default), Date added, Document date, Title (A to Z) and Correspondent (A to Z, documents without one last). The choice is remembered for the person's profile in `profile_view_preferences`, so every device opens in the same order; if it cannot be saved the sort still applies for now and a small notice says so. A sorted list pages by position, so "Load more" never repeats or skips a document.

**Exporting the list (CSV).** **Export list (CSV)** at the bottom of the rail downloads the list exactly as filtered (search, filters, saved view and sort): title, file name, document date, correspondent, type, tags, archive number, one column per custom field the person can see, and the day it was added. It lists only documents the person can see, and never the words inside them or the notes. The file is UTF-8 with a byte-order mark so spreadsheets read accents correctly, one row per document with `CRLF` line ends, and values that begin with `=`, `+`, `-`, `@`, a tab or a carriage return get a single quote in front so they are read as text, never as a formula. At most 10,000 rows are exported; a longer list is cut and a plain notice says "Only the first 10,000 documents were exported. Narrow the list with filters or a saved view to export the rest." There is no scheduled export and no ZIP of the files.

**Documents Inbox and auto-filing rules.** The first choice in the Documents filter rail is **Inbox** with a count ("12 to file"); next is **All documents**, which stays the default, and in the person's own space a third choice, **Trash**, lists documents moved to the trash (Select works in all three). The View menu's Documents tab shows the person's own Inbox count as a badge only when it is above zero. In Inbox mode each result is an Inbox card: thumbnail, title, detected date ("Detected …", or "File date …" when no date was found in the words and the file's time is shown instead), and chips for the correspondent, type, suggested tags and, when a custom field named Amount exists, the suggested amount. A value a rule already filled in is shown instead of the suggestion. **File** accepts the card in one tap (Enter does the same on a computer when the card is focused) and sends exactly what the card shows (the suggestions as displayed, minus any cleared or switched off, plus typed values), so a suggestion that arrives while the card is open is never filed unseen; tapping the correspondent or type chip opens a small dialog to pick or type another name or clear it, tapping a tag chip switches it off or on, and "Add tag" adds one; **File without changes** only marks it filed. Suggestions are shown, never applied silently, except by a rule the person made. Filing works on phones in the person's own space; television and other people's spaces are read-only. Documents in the Shared Library show in its Inbox to everyone who can open it. A household administrator or the server administrator files them with the same **File** and **File without changes** actions, so a Shared Inbox can be emptied; everyone else, including child profiles, sees those cards read-only with the note "Filed by a household administrator". Bulk editing skips Shared documents with a reason on each, except **Mark filed**, which administrators can use on them. An empty Inbox says "Your Inbox is empty." A document is filed on arrival only when a matching rule files automatically; other matching rules leave it in the Inbox with their values filled in. Rules are made on the **Auto-filing rules** page (`/view/documents/rules`, the button in the Documents header on computers and tablets; everyone with Documents reaches their own, phones and tablets see a read-only list with "Edit on a computer", televisions get a plain "not available here" page) and the same list also sits in Settings > Libraries > View for administrators: a plain list (name, a sentence saying what it does, on/off switch, up and down buttons for the running order, edit, delete), "Add rule" and "Apply rules to existing documents", with an editor dialog (name, enabled, all/any, conditions: words contain, file name contains, folder contains, came from a source, file kind; actions: add tag, set correspondent, set type, set field, file automatically; "Test on my documents" lists up to ten matches). On phones and tablets the rule list is read-only with "Edit on a computer". Rules never replace a value a person set, including one they cleared. Document words, notes and amounts appear only on the Documents page, never in Home, global Search or Recommendations. Home's Recently Added lists photos and videos from View only: a document's title, file name or id never appears there, for anyone, and Places and People discovery skip documents too. See [How documents are filed](../explanation/how-documents-are-filed.md).

### View Trash and Empty Trash

Photos' **Trash** filter and Documents' **Trash** (in the person's own space only) start with a header: "Items in Trash stay on your disk until you empty Trash.", then the size line ("128 items · 4.2 GB") on every device. On a computer (`EditorDeviceRule.CanEdit`) with something in Trash, a red **Empty Trash…** button sits beside it. On a phone or tablet the header shows "Empty Trash on a computer." instead, with no button. Empty Trash empties the whole Trash, photos and documents together, one Trash per Personal Space, and its confirmation dialog lists photos, videos and documents separately. Empty Trash appears only in the person's own space (Mine), so Shared Library and other people's spaces never show it. Items in a read-only (linked) folder never show **Move to Trash**, and a mixed selection says how many were left out. Phones keep **Move to Trash** and **Restore**.

## Settings

Settings opens at `/settings` with one sidebar. The **Personal** group is for everyone and opens on the person's Overview (`/settings/overview`, also where the bare `/settings` address and the profile menu's *My profile* go); it lists Overview (with Activity under it), Profiles (administrators only), Preferences (Playback, Listening, Reading) and Security (Devices), in that order. **Server**, **Manage** and **Developer** follow for the people allowed to see them. Old addresses redirect: `/settings/profile` and `/settings/household` go to Profiles and `/settings/account` goes to Security. Devices is a page under Security, not a Server page: `/settings/devices`, `/settings/apps` and `/settings/network/apps` no longer exist and do not redirect.

**Settings > Overview.** `/settings/overview` is the person's own page and the first Personal entry (a Kids profile never reaches Settings, so it has no Overview). It shows the signed-in profile only: a header with their avatar, name, *Joined {Month Year}*, **Edit profile** and a small phone icon (*Apps*) that opens the Apps page; then a row of counts (*In progress*, *Finished*, and the time watched, read and listened in hours, each left out when the Engine has no total for it rather than shown as zero). The counts are the person's lifetime totals from `GET /api/v1/activity/summary`, not a sample of their latest items; then **Recent activity**, a full-width strip and the first content row; then **Your taste**, a one-line summary of the top genres and moods with a *See your taste* link (`/settings/overview/taste`). There is no Manage profiles button, no separate My list row and no star ratings or likes on the page. Edit profile opens the profile editor sheet in its self-edit form: a person changes only their own name and picture (a colour or a photo), saved through the Engine's self-service routes; the Kids switch, content limit, PIN and icons stay with administrators on the person's page in Profiles. The page works on a phone: counts in two columns, buttons full width and the strip scrolling sideways. Photos (View) are not counted or shown. Page: `OverviewPage`; strip: `RecentActivityStrip` and `RecentActivityTile`; wording and marks: `PersonalActivityPresentation`.

**Settings > Overview > Recent activity.** Every tile is the same height and keeps its natural width by shape (`portrait` for books and comics, `landscape` for episodes and movies, `square` for music and audiobooks). Each shows its artwork, a thin progress bar along the bottom for a watched, read or listened item part way through (the shared `AppArtworkProgress`), a check instead of the bar once it is finished, and no bar for music or My list. Under it is one non-wrapping caption that starts with a verb so it cannot be read as the length of the item: *Watched 52 m*, *Read 1 h 20 m*, *Listened 3 h 12 m* (audiobooks), *Played 1 h 05 m* (music) or *Added to My list*. Titles are not printed. Music is one **music session** tile, a square collage of up to nine album covers (a different nine on each load) whose hover card says how many songs from how many albums. On a screen that can hover, hovering or focusing a tile opens a card built on `AppPopover` with a larger picture, the title, the episode or chapter, when it happened and how long; on a phone or any screen without hover there is no card and a tap opens the item (a music session, or an item whose record no longer points at anything, opens the Activity page, `/settings/overview/activity`). *See all* opens the Activity page too. With nothing yet the strip says *Nothing yet — what you watch, read and listen to shows up here.* The pictures are private to the person, so the browser loads them from the Dashboard route `/engine-activity-artwork/{key}`, which asks the Engine as the signed-in person.

**Settings > Overview > Activity.** `/settings/overview/activity` (the first child of Overview in the menu, before Taste; `/my-activity` is the same page outside Settings for a Kids profile and quick access, always the signed-in person's own activity and always in the normal app layout) is the one list of everything the person watched, listened to, read and added to My list, newest first. Each row shows the time, the picture, the title with its episode (*S2 E4 · Fear*) or artist, what happened (*Watched*, *Listened*, *Read*, *Played* for a music session, *Added to My list*), how long, how far (the percent reached), how it ended (*Finished* or *Stopped*) and the device; a tap opens the item. No IP addresses, sign-in details, ratings or likes appear. Above the list: a search box (title and device; in a scope that includes other people also the person), type (Watched, Listened, Read, Added to My list), a from and to date, outcome (Any, Finished, Not finished), device, and the grouping *By day*, *By week*, *By month* or *By title* (the shared `AppTimelineGroupingControl`, extended with options), with **Show every session** (on in *By title* only) turning the title groups into a flat list. The filters show as removable chips (`AppActiveFilterSummary`); on a phone they sit behind one **Filters** button in a bottom sheet (`BrowseFilterSheet`). *By title* gives one entry per title that expands to its attempts, for example *Started Sep 6, 30 m, stopped at 31% · Sep 9, 12 m, stopped at 43% · Sep 13, finished, 1 h 36 m in total*. A music session is one row with its collage and a **Show songs** button that asks the Engine for that session's songs. A slim **date rail** beside the list (`AppTimelineNavigator`, the one the View photos pages use) shows years opening to months with counts; it follows the filters and the scope, jumping to a period restarts the list at the end of that period (a chip *From Sep 2026 back* clears it), and on a phone it becomes the *Jump to period* picker. **Load more** pages the list with the Engine's cursor, and the next page also loads by itself as the person scrolls near the end (the button stays for keyboards, remotes and screen readers; after a failed page it stops loading by itself until the person presses Load more again). **Export** downloads a CSV of exactly the current filters through the Dashboard route `/engine-activity-export`, which passes on only the filters (never paging or grouping). An administrator also gets a **Scope** control: a server administrator chooses Me, Everyone, a household or one person; a household administrator chooses Me, a person in their household or the household. Other scopes add a **Person** column, and only a server administrator sees Export for them. The Engine decides what any scope may show, logs a server administrator opening someone else's activity, and asks for the administrator PIN when one is set (the page then shows the PIN pad, and asks again after it is entered). A link such as *View activity* on a person's page opens the page with `?scope=profile:{id}`; a scope the viewer could not choose is ignored. Only a server administrator sees the **...** menu with **Delete history...**: choose whose history and a date, see how many entries would go, type DELETE and confirm; members and household administrators have no delete control. Page: `ActivityPage` (rows: `ActivityRow`); wording and date maths: `ActivityListPresentation`.

**Settings > Overview > Taste.** `/settings/overview/taste` (a child of Overview in the Personal group; the breadcrumb reads Personal, Overview, Taste) shows what the Library has learned about a person's taste in three plain groups, **Genres**, **Moods** and **Eras**, each value a chip. This layout is a placeholder until the taste design lands; who sees what will not change with it. On your own taste each chip has an X that hides it, and each group has an **Add** control (free text, with known values offered as small chips that narrow as you type; an era is a decade such as 1990s). A value you typed shows *Added by you*. Nothing you do rewrites what the Library learned: removals and additions are kept separately and applied when taste is shown, and the Overview's one-line *Your taste* summary follows them. A summary, the media mix and when it was last worked out sit above the groups. Other people's taste is always read-only (no X, no Add). A **Scope** filter appears for administrators only: a server administrator can pick Me, Everyone, a household or a person; a household administrator can pick Me, their own household or a person in it. It only offers choices; the Engine decides what each person may see and refuses the rest, and opening someone else's taste is logged (`taste.viewed_others`, once per 30 minutes per actor and scope). A member sees no filter and only their own taste. The page works on a phone. Page: `OverviewTastePage`; Engine actions: `/api/v1/taste`.

**Settings > Overview > Apps.** `/settings/overview/apps` (no menu row; the breadcrumb reads Personal, Overview, Apps) is what a person needs to connect a phone or TV, and every signed-in person sees it. **Get the app** lists a button for each store that has a link in `app_store_links` (`app_store`, `google_play`, `roku`) in `config/ui/global.json`; all three ship blank, so nothing shows until an app is published. **Server address** shows *At home* and, once a secure outside address is set up, *Away from home*, each with Copy (from `GET /network/app-addresses`; an administrator without an outside address gets a link to Remote Access, anyone else is told to ask the server administrator). The **QR code** holds exactly one address and nothing else, never a key or a sign-in: the outside address when it is set, otherwise the one at home, with a label saying which. Scanning it fills in the app's server field; the person still signs in as usual.

**Settings > Profiles.** Profiles is an editing page for administrators (a server administrator, or a household administrator for their own household); a member never sees it in the menu, and opening `/settings/profiles` as a member lands on their first available page. A member changes their own name, picture and details from Edit profile on their Overview and looks after sign-in under Security. The page is the people list and nothing else: no card or stats for the open profile (those belong to the Overview). One 56 px row per person shows their avatar, name, their sign-in email in a small line under the name (or *No sign-in*; read-only here, because a server administrator attaches and removes sign-ins in Users and Sign-in), badges (*You*, *Household administrator*, *Server administrator*, *Kids*, *PIN*) and an **Edit** button that opens that person's own page at `/settings/profiles/{profileId}`. People are grouped under their household's name; a household administrator sees only their own household, and a server administrator sees every household, their own first. The last row of each group is *Add profile* (it opens the editor sheet and adds to that group's household) while the household has fewer than eight people; at eight the Engine refuses and its sentence is shown as it is. A server administrator also gets **Add household**, which opens the Households page (under Users and Sign-in) with the name box ready. Each group's header carries **Export this household** (computer or tablet only, never on a phone; a household administrator sees it for their own household, a server administrator for every one) with the line "Download a file with this household's profiles, their sign-ins, progress, bookmarks and favourites. Anyone with this file can sign in as these people. Keep it somewhere safe." It asks to confirm it's you, then downloads a `.zip` with a short busy state; a failure is one plain sentence (the Engine's own when it gives one, otherwise "The file couldn't be made. Try again.", or "The file couldn't be saved. Try again." when the browser refuses it). There is no "Users in your household" panel: household administrators look after profiles, not users. There is no Switch action anywhere on this page: switching profile happens only in the top-bar profile menu. Kids profiles never reach Settings: the links are hidden, and a typed address or old link to any `/settings` page sends a Kids profile to Home. The picker's *Manage profiles* button and the account menu's *Manage profiles* item open this page for administrators (a television keeps managing in place on the picker, which also still honours `?manage=1` and `?add=1`).

**Settings > Users and Sign-in > Households.** `/settings/access/households` is the Households page, a child of Users and Sign-in in the Manage group and open to server administrators only (a household administrator sees neither the menu row nor the page). The old `/settings/profiles/households` address was retired with no redirect. It lists every household with its name and how many people and sign-ins it holds, and offers **Add household** (a name), **Rename** and **Remove** on each. Remove asks first; the Engine refuses while the household still has people, sign-ins or shared photos, and its plain sentence is shown as it is. While the server administrator's own sign-in has no email and password (or passkey), the Engine refuses a second household: the page shows its sentence (*Set an email and password for your own sign-in first. A Library with several households must not be open to anyone who reaches it.*) with a **Set an email and password** button that opens Settings > Security. With one household the Library may still be used without passwords. 

**Settings > Playback.** Playback is about watching only: *How you watch.* shows Autoplay next episode, Resume where you left off, and Skip back and Skip forward, each saved as soon as it changes, and nothing the player does not honour (there is no default-speed row). Below those, the **Subtitles & audio** group (the `#subtitles` anchor, which Settings > Profiles > a person > Subtitles opens) sets Audio language and Subtitle language (a common-language list; a saved language that is not in it stays selectable) and Show subtitles (*Auto (only when audio is not my language)*, *Always* or *Off*), and the **Subtitle appearance** card shows a dark sample frame with one subtitle line that follows Text size (Small, Medium or Large) and the Background switch as they change; subtitle position and style are not shown. An administrator can open `/settings/playback?profile={id}` to edit another household profile; the page then reads *How (name) watches.* with that profile's avatar, a Settings > Profiles > name > Playback breadcrumb and a link back to the profile, and it shows "You can't change this person's settings." with no controls when the Engine refuses. If the Engine cannot be reached when the page opens, it shows "Playback settings could not be loaded." with a Retry button and no controls (it never shows defaults that a change could save over the profile's real choices). Each change re-reads the profile's current settings and changes only that one field before saving, so a change made on another device in the meantime is kept; if that re-read fails, the page shows the error and saves nothing.

**Settings > Listening.** *How you listen.* groups **Skip** (Skip back 10/15/30 s, Skip forward 15/30/60 s, Resume rewind 0/5/10/15/30 s) and **Sleep timer** (Default length from the profile's sleep timer lengths, or Off, and an Offer end of chapter switch), each saved as soon as it changes; it has no speed, skip-silence, crossfade or output rows. Administrators open `/settings/listening?profile={id}` for another household profile with the same heading, breadcrumb, back link and refusal message as the Playback page. It loads and saves the same way as Playback: an unreachable Engine shows an error with Retry and no controls, and each change is merged into a fresh read of the profile's settings.

**Settings > Reading.** *How you read.* sets the profile's reading defaults, with a live sample paragraph at the top that follows the chosen theme, typeface, text size and line spacing, then **Look** (Theme: Light, Sepia, Dark or Match app; Typeface: Book's own, Serif or Sans; Text size 12 to 32 px; Line spacing 1.2 to 2.4) and **Layout** (Page width: Narrow, Standard or Wide; Layout: Paginated or Scrolled), each saved as soon as it changes. Administrators open `/settings/reading?profile={id}` for another household profile with the same heading (*How (name) reads.*), breadcrumb, back link and refusal message as the Listening page, and the same load error with Retry and merge-on-save behaviour.

**Settings > Security.** `/settings/security` is short row lists, not a wall of cards. *Account details* starts with **Add a password** (status "Anyone who can reach Tuvima can use this account until you do", opening the Secure your account form) only while the account works only on this computer; the Security entry in the Settings navigation carries an attention dot in that same state until the person chooses Not now on the profile menu card; after that the dot is gone and the page starts with a quiet note ("Your account has no password, so it only works at home. Add one any time below."). ## Shared editor and Universe graph rules

- Treat structural media placement, authored-container canonical title/order, and the knowledge graph as separate models. Stage 2 establishes canonical Work identity; bounded Stage 3 enriches grounded entities, qualified graph links, and graph-owned artwork without silently changing authored containers or structural placement.
- **Editing is desktop-only.** One rule, `EditorDeviceRule.CanEdit`, decides: the form factor is desktop AND the browser reports a fine, hover-capable primary pointer (`pointer: fine` and `hover: hover`). Phone, tablet, TV, a native app shell, an unknown form factor and a browser that cannot report its pointer all mean no, so an iPad asking for the desktop site cannot edit while a touch laptop with a mouse can. The browser snapshot (`detectDeviceSnapshot`, refreshed on resize and when a pointer is plugged in or removed) and the server circuit (`DeviceContextService.CanEdit`) feed the same rule, and every Dashboard edit handler refuses when it is false, not only the hidden buttons: the media, collection and gallery launchers return `NotAvailableOnThisDevice` with the toast "Edit on a computer", Editing Mode will not start, a pencil will not open and a field will not save. No Edit, Correct, Rematch, Edit artwork, New/Edit Gallery, New Collection or New Smart Playlist control renders off the desktop, and a window dragged to tablet width leaves Editing Mode by itself. Phones, tablets, TVs, cars and native apps are consumption-only in the Dashboard: no pencils, no Edit button, no drawer and no Dashboard edit routes. The Engine never sees the device: its permission check still decides who may edit, and it does not block coarse-pointer or touch devices. The phone More sheet may keep one disabled row reading "Edit on a computer" where Edit sat; tablets show nothing about editing, and a menu that held only Edit is removed. Needs Review stays a desktop workflow (phones keep its summary only; Review off the desktop does nothing).
- **Editing on the details page (the universal editor).** A person with edit permission, on a device that can edit, sees **Edit** in the page's "..." menu; choosing it shows a sticky editing bar (**Editing**, the tools Details, Artwork and Match, **Cancel** and **Save changes**) and a right-hand panel beside the page. The state lives on the page: it ends on Done, Cancel or leaving the page, and a deep link never starts in edit mode. Nothing is saved while editing: every change is staged, shown on the page at once, counted on the Save button ("Save 3 changes") and listed in **Review changes**, which opens when Save is pressed and saves only on **Save N changes**. Provider facts are read-only with the provider's name and a Reset to provider value where a local value exists. The same bar and panel serve every way in: a title, an album track, an episode, several ticked rows (songs, tracks, episodes, library tables: the bar shows **N selected** and the panel is titled "Edit N songs"), Needs Review (Review opens the item's own page on the Match tool and a saved match closes its review items), Recently Added and library lists (Edit opens the item's page in edit mode), a song's "..." menu or Edit button (album page, that song ticked, Edit panel open) and the player's lyrics button (the album page behind the player). There is no pop-up editor for a single item and no drawer. Moving a song, episode or audiobook part is always match first; a book, comic or movie is never moved into a Series by hand. Esc closes the panel, focus returns to the control that opened it, and unsaved input is guarded: Cancel and leaving the page ask "Unsaved changes". Single people, Universes, series shelves and curated collections keep their own editor dialogs until they move over.
- **One write-back control.** Whether a save also updates the files on disk is the library's **Save changes into the files** setting in Settings > Libraries (per library, identical for every item type and every way in). The editor never offers a second switch: Review changes shows a small read-only line ("Files will be updated: On" or "Off") with a **Change in Settings** link, and only when the library allows it a **Update the files when this is saved** tick box for that one save. Unticking it saves the changes to the library and leaves the files alone.
- The normal media editor uses one stable parent header with adjacent child selectors and a single highlighted editing target. Keep audiobook chapter-title overrides under Chapters. Match information belongs at the top of Details, never in header/selector badges; inherited canonical identity needs no status label. Retail Match owns provider identity plus proposed structural placement; preview previous/target paths before Apply. Do not expose Change Type in the normal editor or Review Queue.
- Universe/entity editing remains content within `SharedMediaEditorShell`, opened from the authorized Edit actions on the Universe Explore surface or its selected entity. Media detail pages link actual accessible Universe relationships; media editors contain no Universe shortcut or mode switch. Keep the Universe header stable and preserve the existing dirty-state guard for category/entity navigation.
- The Universe explorer (`/universe/{Qid}/explore`) works on phones: the graph is a full-width canvas at most 70% of the viewport height with pinch-zoom, drag-pan and 44 px Zoom in, Zoom out, Fit and Reset buttons, and on every device a label-sorted list of every entity (type and relationship count, one real button per row) sits beside or under it, so a keyboard, remote or screen reader can reach each node and open the same detail panel a graph tap opens.
- Follow server-readable section capabilities; label/filter Organization Members and Event Participants as their own projections without replacing the full Relationships section. Keep the Universe category rail in one row and use keyboard-accessible, anchored, viewport-bounded category/sibling selector popovers.
- Managed EntityAsset artwork is owned by its Universe/entity target. Preserve work-scoped role/context/anchor/time/spoiler qualifiers and provenance. Do not conflate real-world dates, structural order, in-universe narrative time, and editor History.

## Vault mode and the Vault (Dashboard)

- **Where the Engine says yes, and only there.** The Dashboard asks the Engine whether the signed-in profile can use Vault mode. Vault mode belongs to the device session: pressing Escape twice, the menu check, switching profile, signing out and closing the app all switch it off and return to Home, and it switches itself off after the idle time while real use of the page keeps it on.

The old chapter-by-chapter reader and the `?reader=new` switch are gone; reading statistics were not carried over (in-book text search was brought back, see Search below).

- **Tools.** The top bar of the new reader has Search in this book, Contents, Bookmark this page, Bookmarks and highlights, and Reading settings. Each opens one panel: a side panel on tablets and desktops, a bottom sheet on phones (about three quarters of the height at most), closed by the scrim, its Close button or Escape.
- **Search.** The magnifier in the top bar (or Ctrl or Cmd + F while the book has focus, since the browser's own Find cannot see inside a book) opens a panel with a search box. Press Enter or the magnifier with two or more letters and the reader looks through every page of the book on the device, ignoring capital letters and accents. Each place found shows the contents entry it sits under and the words around it with the match marked; choosing one closes the panel and goes there, and the matches on the pages shown stay outlined until the next search. The list stops at 50 places and says so; a search with no matches says so plainly. Nothing is sent anywhere.
- **Highlights.** Selecting text shows a small toolbar under the selection with five colours and "highlight and add a note". Tapping an existing highlight opens a card with its colour, its note, Delete and Done; closing the card saves a changed note. Highlights are saved per profile, anchored by a standard EPUB position (CFI range), so they stay on the same words when the text size or width changes. Highlights made in the old reader are not shown. Devices without text selection (a TV remote) can still see highlights and use bookmarks, contents and settings.
- **Bookmarks.** "Bookmark this page" saves the current place with the section name and percent as its label; a place that is already bookmarked says so instead of adding a duplicate. The list opens a bookmark, newest first, and deletes it.
- **Settings.** Theme (light, dark, sepia, auto), text size, line spacing, typeface (the book's own, serif, sans), page width (narrow, standard, wide) and layout (pages or one scroll). They apply immediately. The reader opens with the profile's reading defaults (`/settings/reading`), setting by setting: a setting the person changed in the reader on this device wins over the profile value, and a setting never changed on this device uses the profile value (so a later change to the profile still reaches it). A change in the reader is remembered on the device and also saved to the profile a moment later, and only the setting that changed is written to either. **Reset to my defaults** in the reader's settings panel clears this device's choices, including any saved before this feature, and shows the profile's values again.
- **Keyboard and remote.** Arrow keys, Page Up/Down and Space turn pages while the page has focus; while a toolbar, panel or card has focus, the arrow keys are left to it so a remote can move between controls. Enter or OK on the page shows or hides the bars. Escape closes the card, then the panel, then leaves the book.
- **Phones and TVs.** Bars respect safe areas, panels become bottom sheets under 600px, and controls grow on screens 1600px and wider.
- **Nothing spills past the page.** On reflowable books every element is held to its page column: wide tables and fixed-width blocks shrink to fit, long unbroken words and dotted leaders wrap, and `white-space: nowrap` is set back to normal, so a printed contents list on a front page never leaves a clipped continuation on the next page. Fixed-layout books are left exactly as published. `scripts/visual-qa/book-reader/run-checks.mjs` measures this at 390, 820, 1280 and 1920 px.
