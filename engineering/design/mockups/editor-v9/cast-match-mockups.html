Correct the person a title's cast points to

Draft mockups for Editor v9, cast match (issue #1244), redrawn after Shy's note: the person page is right; the wrong part is which person a title's credit was matched to. So the fix starts on the title (a film, show or episode), in its editor, and changes only that title's credit. Dark only; square pills; no toasts. Example: the cast row "Chris Evans" on Captain America: The First Avenger points to a radio presenter instead of the actor.

A. How you get thereB. People tabC. Change personD. Staged and ReviewE. StatesF. Phone

A. Getting there from the title's page

The film's own page, Cast and crew. Two ways in: the tile's "..." menu, or Edit then the People tab.

  1. 1Cast and crew on the title's page, as today: person tiles with a headshot, name and role.
  2. 2The "..." menu on a tile gains Change person (for people who can edit). Open person is the tile's normal tap.
  3. 3Change person opens the editor over the page, straight to the People tab with this credit's change step open. It is a menu action, never a deep link.
  4. 4Remove from this title is staged too and goes through Review changes.

B. The editor's People tab on the film

Desktop, 1280 wide. Every credit on this title, with who each one is matched to.

  1. 1The same header and footer as every item, and a new tab, People, after Match. (Q120: a tab or part of Match.)
  2. 2One row per credit: headshot, name as it came from the file or provider, role and character, and the person it is matched to.
  3. 3A match to check is flagged amber ("Check") when Wikidata does not list this title for that person. It is a hint, never an alarm.
  4. 4Change person on a row opens screen C for that credit only.

C. Change person for this credit

Desktop. Same shape as every Match step; the bar says which title the person is judged against.

  1. 1Two boxes: name (filled in, not searched until Search) or a pasted Wikidata link.
  2. 2"This title" bar: the film, its year and the role, because the title is the evidence. Results that Wikidata links to this film rank first.
  3. 3Results: headshot, name, Wikidata's one-line description and years. The current match is tagged "Current".
  4. 4The card compares this title's credit with Wikidata: name, whether Wikidata lists this film, role, born.
  5. 5"Only this title changes" is stated in plain words: the person page and every other title keep their match.
  6. 6Use this person stages the change. If that Wikidata person is already in your Library, the credit simply points to them; nothing is merged.

D. Staged, then Review changes

Desktop. Left: the People tab after Use this person. Right: the Review step.

  1. 1The row shows the new person with an amber "Not saved yet" chip and "Undo". The page behind does not change.
  2. 2Review changes names the film, the credit, old and new person, and says other titles are not changed.
  3. 3After Save the title shows the normal Updating state. A re-scan or refresh keeps this choice (a locked credit).

E. Other states

Desktop, the change step.

  1. 1Nothing found: a plain sentence and a suggestion to paste a link.
  2. 2Wikidata not answering: the current person is unchanged.
  3. 3No permission: the People tab lists credits and matches, with no Change buttons and one line saying why.
  4. 4Credit not matched at all: "Not matched" chip and a Find person button that opens the same search.

F. On a phone

360 wide. Full-screen sheet, one-line header.

  1. 1Rows stack: headshot, name, role, matched person, Change person as a full-width button under a flagged row.
  2. 2Search fields stack; Search is full width; typed text is 16 px.
  3. 3Tapping a result opens its card as a full-height sheet; Use this person stays at the bottom.
