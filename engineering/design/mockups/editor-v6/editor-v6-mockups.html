Editor v6 mockups

The Match view redrawn to the target picture, the aligned rows, and the editor on a phone. Each screen shows what the app will show. The numbered dots are not part of the app; they point to the notes under each screen.

Covers are colored placeholders. Provider marks are drawn as letter badges here; the real marks go in where each provider's brand rules allow.

A. Match a bookB. Already matchedC. TV episodeD. SongE. WikidataF. Details rowsG. Series chooserH. Phone

A. Match a book that is not matched yet

Desktop, 1280 wide. After pressing Search.

  1. 1One-line header on Match. Small cover, "Match book", and the state. Home and the item list stay at the right so you can move to the next book without leaving Match.
  2. 2The editor's own tabs stay. This row is not in the target picture; it is how you get back to Details, Artwork and History.
  3. 3Media providers and Wikidata. Drawn as a two-way selector, not a second underlined row, so two tab rows never sit on top of each other. It is on its own line, so the search row below has the full width.
  4. 4One search row. Every control is 44 px high with its label above. The provider shows its mark and name, and a state only when something is wrong.
  5. 5Message line. Help, status and errors for the row go here, under the whole row, so they cannot push a control down.
  6. 6Your file. The file name, then what the file itself says.
  7. 7Results. No pill and no description. The tick marks the chosen one; box sets say "Collection".
  8. 8The chosen result. Large cover to compare with your own, two lines of description, then Read more.
  9. 9Verdict and table. Strong, Possible or Weak match. A tick where the two agree, a dash where one side has nothing. A dash never counts against the match.
  10. 10Apply match only stages the change. Save changes in the footer writes it, after Review.

B. An item that is already matched

Desktop. A movie. Nothing is searched when the tab opens.

  1. 1Green dot and the provider's name. This is the state that wrongly read "Not matched yet".
  2. 2The same card as screen A, filled with the current match, at full width.
  3. 3Runtime. Your file's length is measured; the provider's is rounded. Within two minutes counts as agreeing.
  4. 4Three actions. Change match shows the search row and list from screen A. Remove match is staged like any change.

C. Match a TV episode

Desktop. The show is already chosen; this is the episode step.

  1. 1The show you chose, with Change show. TV uses TheTVDB only, so the provider is shown, not offered as a choice.
  2. 2Season, then a plain list of that season's episodes.
  3. 3Suggested from the file name. The episode the file name points to is chosen for you.
  4. 4The card uses a wide picture for an episode. The table compares show, number, title and length.

D. Match a song

Desktop. The release is chosen on the left, the track on the right.

  1. 1Fields for a song: Artist, Album, Song.
  2. 2Releases. The one whose track count and lengths fit your files is suggested first.
  3. 3Tracks of the chosen release. The track that fits is chosen for you.
  4. 4Possible match. The length differs by more than three seconds, so it says so in the table.
  5. 5What will change when the song belongs to a different album than it does today.

E. The Wikidata side

Desktop. Same shape, text only.

  1. 1Two boxes: search by name, or paste a Wikidata link or ID.
  2. 2Text results: name, what it is, and one fact that tells it apart.
  3. 3A series cannot stand in for a book. It is marked as the parent and cannot be applied.
  4. 4The table compares identifiers both sides know, then title, author and year.

F. Details tab: rows that line up

Desktop. The full header stays on every tab except Match.

  1. 1Sort title and Rating share one line. Labels above, controls on one line, whatever sits under either.
  2. 2A note under one field ("Goes back to the provider's value when you save") sits on the message line under the whole row. Rating does not move.
  3. 3No Read-only or Write-back pills. The footer line says it once.
  4. 4The file path is out of the facts list. It shows once at the foot of Quick Facts on one line, shortened in the middle, with the full path on hover and a copy button.
  5. 5Outlined icons for Home and every icon in the header and tab row.

H. The editor on a phone

360 wide. The same editor as a full-screen sheet.

  1. 1Always the one-line header. Tapping it opens the navigation sheet (screen 4).
  2. 2Fields stack, one per line, and Search is full width. Typed text is 16 px so the phone does not zoom. Author and ISBN sit under Title the same way; they are left out of this drawing so the results fit.
  3. 3Tapping a result opens its card as a full-height sheet (screen 2).
  4. 4Apply match stays at the bottom of the card, under the thumb. Back returns to the list.
  5. 5Tap the lock to unlock a field. Tap the lock again for the reason it was locked, or to go back to the provider's value.
  6. 6Quick Facts is closed under the form; tap to open.
  7. 7Footer: the write-back line, then Discard and Save changes. Delete is in the "..." menu at the top.
  8. 8Navigation sheet: Home, Season, the episodes with their pictures, then supporting files and the folder button.
