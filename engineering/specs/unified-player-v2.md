# Spec: Unified player v2 (one player system for music, audiobooks, TV and movies)

> **Status:** Locked (Shy, 2026-11-07 07:01Z) · **Version:** 2 (supersedes [Unified player](unified-player.md) v1; amends the specs listed in section 10) · **Last updated:** 2026-11-07 07:05Z · **Related docs:** [Player controls v3](player-controls-v3.md) · [Video player controls and bookmarks](video-player-controls-and-bookmarks.md) · [Music player and queue](music-player-and-queue.md) · [Global bookmarks](global-bookmarks.md) · [Look pass v2](look-pass-v2.md) (D6 tooltip, A13 players) · [Look v2 ownership map](look-pass-v2-ownership.md) · [Design system](../design/design-system.md) · Shy's brief and 13 reference images (local check thread, 2026-11-07 04:29Z) · Mockups: [design/mockups/unified-player-v2.html](../design/mockups/unified-player-v2.html) (16 numbered screens)

## 1. Goal

Today there are three players that happen to share a few parts: the music and audiobook bar (`ListenNowPlayingBar`, about 2,240 lines), the video host (`VideoPlaybackHost`, about 1,680 lines) and the phone full player, each with its own control order, hover rules, pop-up panels and menus. Controls sit in different places per media, pop-ups look different from one another and from the rest of the app, and some still open on hover or hide behind other content.

After this work there is **one player shell** with one control row, one panel system and one set of visual rules, and only the tools on the right change with the media. Playback itself (the Engine session, queue, progress, bookmarks, lyrics, subtitles, permissions) is untouched: this is a presentation rebuild on top of the playback services that already exist.

### Plain-English walkthrough of the finished experience

1. **Something starts playing.** A bar appears at the bottom of the page. Whatever it is, the bar has the same shape: a thin timeline on top, then one row with the artwork and title on the left, Play in the exact middle, and the tools for that kind of media on the right. Movies and TV episodes now show their artwork in the bar too.
2. **The middle never moves.** Audiobooks, TV and movies: Previous, back 10 seconds, Play, forward 30 seconds, Next. Music: Previous, Play, Next, with Shuffle and Repeat just outside, because songs are skipped whole, not by seconds (Previous restarts the song, and a second press, or a press in the first three seconds, goes back one, as in Spotify and Apple Music). Previous and Next mean track, chapter or episode. A movie that has nothing to go to shows the two buttons dimmed, in the same place.
3. **Music gets its personal actions beside the song:** Favorite, Like, Dislike and a "..." menu, with room between them, never pushed into the tools on the right. Shuffle and Repeat sit just outside the middle five.
4. **Every tool opens the same kind of panel:** a near-black panel with an 8 px corner, a fixed title row with a close button, a scrolling middle, and a fixed footer when a panel has actions. No speech-bubble arrows. One panel at a time. Escape, the close button, the tool's own button or a click outside closes it, and focus returns to the button. Panels open above the bar, or inside the video (including fullscreen), and never get clipped.
5. **Watching a video:** one "Audio & subtitles" button opens two columns, Audio on the left and Subtitles on the right, each row with a check when chosen; the footer has "Subtitle appearance" (a nested view with a live preview and three presets) and "Find subtitles…". A separate gear, "Playback settings", holds speed, skip lengths, picture-in-picture and playback information. The duplicate subtitle style button and the duplicate audio and subtitle rows in Settings are gone.
6. **Listening to an audiobook:** Chapters, the speed value (tap it to open the speed panel directly), Sleep timer, Bookmarks, Volume, Now playing. The timeline can show the chapter or the whole book and says which.
7. **Listening to music:** Lyrics, Queue (with Up next and History tabs), Bookmarks, Volume, Now playing. Lyrics follow the song and highlight the line; scrolling by hand pauses the following, with a "Resume following" button.
8. **Now playing** (music and audiobooks) is a full page with the big cover, the facts, and tabs (Lyrics, Queue, History for music; Chapters and book facts for audiobooks), with the same bar at the bottom. Opening and closing it never interrupts playback.
9. **On a phone** the bar collapses to artwork, title, Play and Next (no Favorite); tapping it opens Now playing: big art, title with Favorite and ratings, the five transport buttons and one short row of tools, with everything else in a "..." menu, and every panel is a bottom sheet with its own scrolling.
10. **On the lock screen** (phone, Windows, Mac, headset buttons) the system card shows the same artwork, title and second line as the bar, with Play, Previous and Next for music and back 10 / forward 30 for audiobooks and video. Tuvima fills the card; the system draws it.
11. **Playing on a TV from the Dashboard** is one row, "Play on another device…", in the "..." menu (music, audiobooks) or Playback settings (video). It opens the browser's own AirPlay or Google Cast picker. While playing elsewhere the bar says "Playing on Living room TV" and still controls it.
12. **The TV apps and the car apps** follow the same order, names and defaults as this bar, written once in a player contract that every client reads, so a person who learns the Dashboard already knows the Roku, Android TV, Apple TV, Samsung and LG apps, CarPlay and Android Auto.

### Representative journeys

- **J1 Movie night.** Play a movie from its page. The bar shows the poster, "The Glass Harbor · 2026 · Movie". Previous and Next are dimmed. Open Audio & subtitles, pick "English · SDH": the picture never restarts. Press Fullscreen; open Bookmarks inside fullscreen; add one at the current time with no note.
- **J2 Binge a show.** Play S1 E1. Episodes opens with the season dropdown, the current episode highlighted and "Up next" on E2. Pick E4: it resumes where that episode was left. Autoplay next is a switch in the panel's footer.
- **J3 Audiobook in bed.** Tap "1.25×", choose 1.5× from the presets or nudge the slider in 0.05 steps. Open Sleep timer, choose "End of chapter". Open Now playing, then close it: the timer and position are unchanged.
- **J4 Music while browsing.** Press Like on the bar (Dislike clears if it was set). Open Lyrics, scroll up to re-read a verse, press Resume following. Open Queue, drag a song up, Clear upcoming: the current song keeps playing.
- **J5 Phone.** Collapsed bar at the bottom of every page, never covering content, with Play and Next at hand. Tap to expand; tap Audio & subtitles: a bottom sheet with Audio and Subtitles tabs. Lock the phone: the lock screen shows the cover, the song and the artist with Previous, Play and Next.
- **J6 Living room.** Playing a film in Chrome on a laptop, open Playback settings › "Play on another device…", pick the living-room TV from Chrome's own list. The picture moves to the TV, the laptop goes silent, the bar reads "Playing on Living room TV" and Play, seek, Audio & subtitles and Bookmarks keep working from the laptop. Close the bar, or pick "Stop playing on Living room TV", and it returns.
- **J7 Same player, different room.** Open the same show on the Roku or the Samsung TV: the five transport buttons in the same order, back 10 / forward 30 on the remote's left and right keys, Episodes, Audio & subtitles and Bookmarks in the same order and with the same names as the Dashboard. In the car, CarPlay or Android Auto shows Continue and the Listen shelves, and the Now playing card has Previous, Play and Next for music and back 10 / forward 30 and chapters for audiobooks.

### Scope boundaries

In: the Dashboard player on desktop, tablet and phone, in all four presentation states. Also in: the lock-screen and system-controls contract (D27), Play on another device from the Dashboard (D28), and the **definition** of the TV and car players in one player contract (D29); the code changes inside `clients/` that bring each app to that contract are follow-up client threads, listed in section 9. Out: the book, comic and PDF readers, new metadata or Engine playback features (timeline thumbnail previews, Engine-side audio track re-muxing) beyond what exists, bookmark sharing between named people (section 3 D13).

## 2. Scope

**In scope**
- A shared player shell (`PlaybackShell`) that every presentation state renders through: persistent bar, expanded Now playing (music and audiobooks), video with overlay controls, fullscreen video.
- One control row with fixed transport in the middle, media-specific identity on the left and tools on the right; one set of sizes, hover, pressed, selected, disabled and focus treatments; the player's existing stroke-icon family.
- One timeline and one slider style (timeline, volume, speed, subtitle opacity).
- One panel system for every pop-up: compact menus and larger panels, nested views, bottom sheets on phones.
- The panels: Audio & subtitles (with Subtitle appearance nested), Playback settings, Speed, Sleep timer, Lyrics, Queue (Up next and History), Episodes, Chapters, Bookmarks, Item actions, Volume.
- Now playing expanded views for music and audiobooks.
- Video overlay: paused details, fade rules, panel container in fullscreen, subtitle placement.
- Phone and tablet reflow.
- Guardrail tests, public docs and the Local checks list.

**Out of scope**
- Code changes inside `clients/` (each TV and car app is brought to the player contract of D29 in its own follow-up thread); the book, comic and PDF readers.
- New Engine capabilities beyond the scrub previews and bookmark frames (D9a, D21a): Engine-side audio track switching where the browser cannot (video spec v2 D10 is reused only if it was built; otherwise listed as a follow-up), bookmark sharing between named people.
- The Songs list, the Watch and Listen pages, playlists, and the Settings › Playback page (it keeps its switches; the player's Playback settings panel reads and writes the same preferences).
- A Tuvima-made cast receiver app or a Tuvima remote-control protocol between devices (D28 uses the browser's AirPlay and Google Cast pickers and the Engine's existing cast link; nothing new is installed on the TV).

**Shell and bar**

- **D1. One shell, four states.** One component tree (`PlaybackShell`) hosts one `<audio>` and one `<video>` element for the whole session and renders the bar, the Now playing page, the video overlay and fullscreen from the same state. Switching state never recreates the media element, so position, queue, selected tracks, volume, speed and the sleep timer survive. (Reliability, Maintenance.) Today the music bar and the video host are separate trees that reach the same `PlaybackSessionController`; the shell replaces their duplicated bar, panel and hover code and keeps their playback wiring.
- **D2. Three zones, Play at true center.** The control row is a three-column grid whose outer columns are equal (`1fr auto 1fr`), so the middle cluster is centered no matter how wide the title or the tools are. Titles truncate with an ellipsis; tools never wrap on desktop. At 1024 wide the identity zone keeps at least 240 px.
- **D3. Transport order and meaning (revised 2026-11-07 06:38Z).** Audiobooks, TV and movies: Previous · Skip back · Play/pause · Skip forward · Next, in every state. **Music: Previous · Play/pause · Next**, with Shuffle on the left and Repeat on the right, and no skip-by-seconds buttons: songs are skipped whole, as in Spotify and Apple Music, and the timeline is there for the rare seek. Previous in music follows the convention those apps use: after 3 seconds of play it restarts the current song; within the first 3 seconds, or pressed again within 3 seconds of a restart, it goes to the previous song (at the start of the queue it restarts). The 3-second threshold lives in `config/ui/playback-client.json` next to the skip defaults, not in code. Play stays at the exact center of the bar in both layouts (the music row is three buttons plus two, centred as one group). Previous/Next: tracks (music), chapters (audiobooks), episodes (TV), queue items (movies, when a playlist or queue is playing). When unavailable they are **disabled in place**, never hidden (overrides v1 D2, which hid them for films). Skip back defaults to **10 s** and skip forward to **30 s** for every media that has them, changeable per profile in Playback settings (overrides the 15 s audiobook defaults in `PlaybackControlCatalog` and v1 D2). Music gets Shuffle to the left of Previous and Repeat to the right of Next, outside the five, with Repeat showing Off, Repeat all (ring) and Repeat one (ring with "1"), and a purple selected state with a changed glyph.
- **D4. Tools per media, in this order (right zone):**
  - Music: Lyrics · Queue · Bookmarks · Volume · Now playing · Close (every tool is a 44 px icon button with a tooltip, including Now playing and the audiobook speed value, which is a text chip)
  - Audiobooks: Chapters · Speed (the value, "1.25×") · Sleep timer · Bookmarks · Volume · Now playing · Close
  - TV: Episodes · Audio & subtitles · Bookmarks · Volume · Playback settings · Fullscreen · Close
  - Movies: Audio & subtitles · Bookmarks · Volume · Playback settings · Fullscreen · Close
  A tool shows only when the media and the app support it (no chapters: no Chapters; one audio track and no subtitles: Audio & subtitles still shows, with the single track listed and not selectable, per v3 D4). Shared tools keep their relative order so Volume, Bookmarks and Close are always in the same neighbourhood. Empty space stays empty. Lyrics is always shown for music (music spec D6d). `PlaybackControlCatalog` becomes the single source of this list (it already is for most of it).
- **D5. Identity zone.** Fixed artwork slot: 56 px tall at desktop, square for music and audiobooks, 16:9 landscape (100 × 56) for TV and movies with the episode still or show art, and the poster shown inside a 16:9 slot with a blurred fill when only a poster exists (contain, never stretch). Title (16 / 600) and secondary line (13, muted): music track · artist; audiobook title · author · chapter; TV show · "S1 E1 · episode title"; movie title · year · "Movie". Title and names are links to their pages (`PlaybackIdentityLink` already does this); there is no "View details" button. Music adds Favorite, Like, Dislike and More beside the text with 8 px gaps; audiobooks, TV and movies add Favorite (where the item supports it) and More. Favorite and rating are separate states: Like clears Dislike and vice versa; pressing an active rating removes it (today's `SongActions` semantics).
- **D6. Sizes and states (one token set).** Icons 24 px in 44 px targets; Play/pause 32 px glyph in a 48 px fixed target. Hover: translucent white background (8% on the bar, 12% over video), no scaling, no glow ring, no underline (overrides v1 D3 "grow, no background" and v3 D2; overrides video v2 D3 accent hover and 3 px underline). Pressed: 16%. Selected (a tool whose panel is open, Shuffle on, Repeat on, subtitles showing, speed not 1×, sleep timer running): purple-soft background plus a changed glyph or check. Disabled: 38% opacity, no hover. Keyboard focus: 2 px purple ring, 2 px offset. Icons are white; secondary text muted; purple only for progress, selected and focus. The player keeps its own **stroke-icon family** (`PlaybackUtilityGlyph`) as Look v2 A4 allows ("stroke glyphs only in the player"); the Material icons that crept into `PlayerSettingsMenu` (back arrow, chevrons) and panel headers are replaced with stroke glyphs. Nothing from MudBlazor or Material is added. These sizes replace the 56 px targets and 38 px glyphs of Player controls v3 D1 (Shy's brief: about 24 px icons in 44 px targets); the player keeps its own size tokens (`--playback-control-target` 44, `--playback-control-glyph` 24, `--playback-play-target` 48, `--playback-play-glyph` 32) and takes every color, border, hover, selected and type value from the shared tokens named by Look v2 and the Look v3 draft (`--tl-bg-surface-elevated`, `--tl-border-default`, `--tl-accent-primary-soft`, `--tl-accent-primary-hover`, 16 / 500 rows, 13 helper, 20 / 600 panel headings).
- **D7. Surfaces (Shy: 1A, neutral).** Panels and menus use the app's menu surface from Look v2 (`--tl-bg-surface-elevated` #2F3039, fine 1 px border, 8 px radius, one restrained shadow); over video a 96% opaque version of the same color, no blue or violet tint (Look v2 A13; bug 13 decided neutral). The bar is the shell color (#14151A) over pages and a bottom gradient over video. Shy's images show a violet-tinted near-black; the brief's text also says "subtle violet tint". Recommendation: neutral per Look v2 so the player matches every other menu in the app; the violet lives only in progress, selected states and focus. If Shy prefers the tint, it becomes one token in the player only (`--playback-panel-tint`).
- **D8. Tooltips.** Every player control uses the app tooltip of Look v2 D6 (150 ms, top layer, no arrow); no browser `title=` hints (A11 guardrail). The Audio & subtitles tooltip reads "Audio & subtitles".

**Timeline and volume**

- **D9. Timeline.** A 4 px neutral rail (white 22%), purple played portion, a 12 px handle with no glow and a 24 px invisible hit area; elapsed on the left and remaining ("−1:49") on the right. Scrubbing shows the target time above the handle and seeks on release; keyboard arrows seek 5 s (Shift: 30 s). Seeking is clamped to the loaded duration. Video shows a thumbnail preview above the handle whenever the Engine has scrub previews for that file (D9a); until it does, the timestamp-only preview shows. Bookmark markers are 2 × 10 px ticks under the rail in the marker colors of the Bookmarks panel's filter, hidden while dragging the handle. Audiobooks label the rail "Chapter 8 · 12:08 · −18:32" in chapter mode and "Book · 32% · 6h 12m left" in whole-book mode, switched from the Chapters panel footer (keeps v1 D5; the small bar switch moves into the panel).
- **D9a. Scrub previews, made ahead of time (Shy, 2026-11-07 06:25Z: in scope).** The Engine generates preview frames for every film, episode and video **ahead of time**, not on demand: a background job of the lowest priority, one file at a time, runs after a video is identified (and gradually over files already in the library), takes one frame every 10 seconds at 320 px wide with FFmpeg, tiles them into sprite sheets of 10 × 10 with a small index file, and stores them per Edition in the Library's own cache (a two-hour film is about 720 frames, roughly 4 to 6 MB). On demand would mean seeking into the file for every hover, which takes a few hundred milliseconds per frame and loads the machine exactly while someone is watching; ahead of time costs disk and a one-off pass per file but makes scrubbing instant. The job never runs while a transcode is being served, and it is one process, so it cannot starve ingestion. A server-side switch under Settings › Playback › Advanced, "Generate scrub previews" (on by default, with the space used shown), turns it off and removes the cache. Previews follow the same access checks as the file (Vault included) and are deleted with the Edition. The bookmark frame of D21a reuses the nearest preview frame when one exists and extracts an exact frame otherwise. The Dashboard reads the index once per playback and shows the right tile above the handle while scrubbing, timestamp underneath; no preview on audio.
- **D10. Volume.** One speaker glyph (three states: loud, quiet, muted with a cross). Click toggles mute; unmuting restores the previous non-zero level (the Dashboard remembers it; the fix for the "starts muted" icon from v1 WP2 stays). On desktop, hover or keyboard focus reveals a **horizontal** 96 px slider to the right of the speaker inside the tools zone, which stays open while the pointer or focus is on it and for 400 ms after; the other tools do not shift because the slider overlays a reserved 0-width slot that expands and pushes nothing (the zone is right-aligned, so the identity zone absorbs the width). On touch, tapping the speaker opens a small volume panel instead of toggling mute; mute is inside it. Overrides v1 D8 and v3 D6 (vertical box).

**Panels**

- **D11. One panel system (`PlaybackPanel`), two sizes.** *Menu* (272 px, 44 px rows, 16 / 500, separators, optional header line with the item name) for Item actions, Repeat, skip lengths; *Panel* (360 to 420 px wide, max 80% of the player or viewport height) for lists and lyrics; Audio & subtitles is a 560 px two-column panel. All open above the bar (or inside the video area in the video states), are drawn in the browser top layer through the shared `AppPopoverHost` so no container clips them, and in fullscreen are rendered inside the fullscreen element. Position: anchored to the trigger, right-aligned to the tools zone, flipped or shifted to stay 12 px inside the viewport. No arrow. Exactly one top-level panel at a time: opening another closes the first. Header (title, optional back, close) and footer actions are fixed; only the middle scrolls, vertically only. Closes on the trigger, the close button, outside click or Escape; focus returns to the trigger. Nested views (Subtitle appearance, Custom appearance, Custom sleep duration, Playback information) slide in with a back arrow and return to their parent. Panels never open on hover (overrides video v2 D4 hover-open; fixes the pinned Speed panel root cause for good because the open state has one owner). Opening a panel never changes playback. The video controls stay visible while a panel is open, and the chrome does not auto-hide while a panel is open, the pointer is over the controls, a control has focus, or a menu is in use. Phones: every panel is a bottom sheet (Look v2 D5): bounded height (max 85%), internal scroll, safe-area padding, drag handle and close, Escape and backdrop tap dismiss.
- **D12. Audio & subtitles (TV, movies).** One button with a combined glyph (speech bubble with sound waves) and the tooltip "Audio & subtitles". Desktop: two columns. Audio rows: language name, then "Dolby Atmos · 7.1" style facts from the file's track data; commentary and audio-description tracks are labelled ("Director commentary", "Audio description") and sorted last. Subtitles: Off, then each track with its label kept ("English · SDH", "English · Forced"). Selected rows: check plus the soft purple background. Audio and subtitles are independent; switching keeps the position (the browser switch for subtitles, the Engine re-stream and resume for audio tracks the browser cannot switch, video spec v2 D10; a track the app cannot switch is listed as not selectable with "Not switchable in this browser"). Footer: "Subtitle appearance" (nested, D13) and "Find subtitles…" (the existing Find another subtitles view, nested). The separate Subtitle style button (v1 D12) leaves the bar; Audio and Subtitles rows leave Playback settings (overrides v3 D4 and v1 D10). Phones: Audio and Subtitles become tabs in one sheet.
- **D13. Subtitle appearance (nested).** Live preview strip at the top over a still from the video (or a neutral image). Style presets Default, Large text, High contrast (tiles with a check). Text size Small / Medium / Large and Background None / Shadow / Box as segmented controls. "Custom appearance › Font, colors and opacity" opens the existing full choices (font, text color, text opacity, background color, background opacity, shadow) as a second nested view. "Reset to default" and "Changes saved automatically" in the footer. Changes apply at once and persist through the existing per-profile preference model (`SubtitleStyleCss`, `PlayerPreferenceService`). A preset sets the underlying values; a custom value shows no preset ticked.
- **D14. Playback settings (gear).** Rows: Playback speed (presets 0.5× to 2× plus "Custom speed…" removed; instead the Speed panel of D15 opens inline), Skip back (5 / 10 / 15 / 30 s), Skip forward (10 / 30 / 60 / 90 s), Picture-in-picture (video, when the browser allows), Playback information (nested view with the existing technical summary: video, audio and connection details), and "Reset playback preferences". These are the same per-profile preferences Settings › Playback held; with Shy's answer 5A the player is their only home, and Settings › Playback keeps only the preferred audio and subtitle language (with Show subtitles), Resume where you left off, Spoilers, and the server-side Quality and Advanced tabs (WP6 removes the duplicated rows from `PlaybackSettingsPage`). ## 8. Risks and open questions

- **Size.** This rebuilds the presentation of three large components. Mitigation: the shell and the panel system ship first and alone (W1), each later wave swaps one panel at a time, and the reviewer runs after each wave. The old components stay until their last panel moves, so each wave is revertable.
- **Engine limits.** Engine-side audio switching (if video v2 D10 was not built) and person-to-person bookmark sharing are not possible without Engine work; the build reports which of these it found missing, and the Local checks say what the browser could not do.
- **Browser audio tracks.** Chrome and Edge do not expose audio tracks; rows the app cannot switch are listed as not switchable rather than hidden, so people can see what the file has.
- **Look v2 tokens.** `--tl-menu-surface` in the token file is still the old violet value; the panel work uses `--tl-bg-surface-elevated` directly so the player does not depend on that cleanup (and reports it to the Look v2 owner).
- **Conflicts with Look v2 A13.** A13 names 16 px radius removal and menu gray; this spec's 8 px radius and neutral surface agree with it. If Q1 chooses the violet tint, A13 needs a one-line amendment.

## 10. Why it works this way (for the public docs)

1. **One bar everywhere:** learn it once; Play is in the middle because it is what you reach for most.
2. **Artwork in every bar:** you see what is playing without reading.
3. **Personal actions beside the song:** Favorite and Like are about the song, so they sit with its name, not with the player's tools.
4. **Panels that never clip and open one at a time:** a menu that hides behind the page or stacks on another is a bug, not a feature.
5. **Audio & subtitles together:** they are chosen together, in two columns, so you see both at once.
6. **Speed presets with a slider:** quick choices for most people, fine steps for the rest, and the number always tells the truth.
7. **Bookmarks are Everyone's or Mine:** the library's definitive set is simply there for every viewer, made by an administrator in the editor; your own stay yours.
8. **Phones reflow rather than shrink:** thumbs need room; the bar collapses so it never covers your library.
9. **Music skips songs, not seconds:** Previous and Next are what every music app taught you; Previous restarts first, then goes back.
10. **The lock screen is the system's:** Tuvima fills in the cover, the names and the right buttons, and lets your phone, PC or car draw them, so it matches everything else you play there.
11. **Your TV is already a receiver:** AirPlay and Google Cast are built into the browser, so Tuvima asks the browser rather than installing anything on the TV; the action sits in a menu because on a desktop it is the exception, not the rule.
12. **One contract for every screen:** the TV apps and the car apps follow the Dashboard's order and names, so learning one player is learning them all.

## Plain English summary

The player becomes one player on every screen Tuvima has, and this plan also says what the lock screen, the TV apps and the car apps show. Whatever is playing, the same bar appears with Play dead center, artwork and title on the left, and only the tools that fit on the right. Every pop-up, from lyrics to subtitles, uses one panel design that opens above the bar, never gets cut off, and closes the same way. Video gets one Audio & subtitles panel and one Playback settings gear; audiobooks get a direct speed panel and a proper sleep timer; music keeps Favorite and Like beside the song and gets a single Queue with Up next and History. Nothing about playback itself changes, and no data is touched; this is a rebuild of what you see and click, done in eight waves so each piece can be checked on its own.
