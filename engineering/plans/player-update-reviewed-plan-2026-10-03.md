## Plain English product walkthrough

1. **The audio bar becomes a permanent bottom edge of the app.** Music and audiobooks use a square-edged, full-width dock, including beneath the lane rail. The progress line sits on its top edge; elapsed time is on the left and negative remaining time on the right. Music adds Favorite and More beside its identity. Desktop transport remains centered even when the side groups have different widths. Desktop and tablet docks have an explicit Close player icon at their far right. At tablet widths, an anchored More menu keeps secondary tools reachable without crowding the transport; Close remains directly visible. A compact phone dock, where the existing browsing shell renders one, keeps identity, Play/Pause, and Expand without a Close action. Acceptance: the dock has no outer gap or rounded floating frame at desktop, tablet, or phone widths; desktop/tablet Close stops playback while preserving resume and history, while phone Collapse returns to this compact dock without stopping the session. Maps to WP1 and WP3; visual references R04, R05, R06, and R12, with desktop/tablet Close added as an intentional required difference.

2. **Opening an audio tool leaves the page in place.** Queue, Lyrics, and History open above their dock buttons on desktop. A queue panel has Playing Next and History tabs, the upcoming count, Clear, and removable rows. Clear keeps the playing song. Audiobooks have one Chapters/History entry, which becomes History when there are no usable chapter choices. The compact chapter panel uses readable title/duration rows, with an outlined current row and playing indicator. Phones use bottom sheets with prominent removal targets. Acceptance: opening and closing a tool changes neither page width nor scroll position, and only one temporary playback tool is open. Maps to WP1, WP3, and WP4; visual references R02, R06, R07, and R11.

3. **Desktop Now Playing follows the new music and audiobook references.** A blurred artwork atmosphere supports large sharp artwork with the identity beneath it in the left region, and a built-in panel on the right. Music offers Lyrics, Queue, and History; audiobooks offer Chapters and History. The bottom dock remains available. Its tool buttons select the corresponding built-in tab. Only real, accessible artist, author, album, book, playlist, and collection identities become links. Acceptance: the artwork, identity, panel, and dock remain readable without overlapping at normal desktop sizes and short laptop heights. Maps to WP4; visual references R08, R09, and R10. The original three-column composition remains secondary inspiration.

4. **Lyrics work consistently in every audio player.** Timed lyrics highlight the current line with stronger text and a purple vertical marker and allow seeking; ordinary text remains readable without pretending to be synchronized. Users retain version selection, preferred-version selection, and finding better lyrics in a compact toolbar above the lyric scroller. Manual scrolling temporarily stops automatic following, with a Back to current line action. Acceptance: switching tracks cannot display an old response, and changing player presentation preserves the selected lyric version for the current track. Maps to WP4 and WP5; visual reference R08.

5. **The phone and separate window share one full player.** Both fill their available screen/window with a vertical composition: generous sharp artwork, identity, a seek rail, centered transport, and a lower utility row. The phone restores R01/R03's top-left downward chevron: Collapse player returns to the library UI and compact audio dock while playback continues. This minimizes the presentation; it does not stop or dismiss the session. The popup has no player Close, Stop, Collapse, Back-to-dock, or window-close action, and no empty header slot for those omitted controls. Its native window close button belongs to the operating system. Music retains square art; audiobooks retain portrait art and identify the active chapter. Both share the same media tools and layout at equal viewport dimensions, with the phone-only Collapse control. The popup defaults to 420 by 780 pixels and fills its content area. Opening or closing its native window preserves main-window audio. Audiobook bookmarks, speed, chapter navigation, history, and sleep keep their current behavior. Whole-book progress becomes text; the real playback position remains seekable where it is today. Acceptance: phone collapse/re-expand preserves the session and makes browsing available, equivalent media actions affect the same session once, and artwork yields space before controls become clipped. Maps to WP1 and WP5; visual references R01, R02, R03, and R04.

6. **Video controls disappear when viewers are watching.** During active playback, controls and cursor fade after three idle seconds. Pointer movement, touch, or keyboard activity brings them back. Pause, loading, errors, focused controls, open tools, and the next-episode card keep controls visible. Back sits at top left; supported PiP and any already-functional Cast action sit at top right. Volume sits at bottom left and tools at bottom right; central skip/play controls remain. Acceptance: idle video shows the picture and captions, and interactions remain keyboard-accessible. Maps to WP1 and WP2; R17 is the controls-visible reference, while the hidden-controls state requires a separate runtime capture.

7. **Video tools open near their buttons.** Desktop Captions, Audio, and the contextual Episodes/Chapters/Up Next panel use small anchored panels without dimming the movie. TV lists accessible owned episodes by season, with the current episode identified. Movies show real chapters when available; other videos show an actual upcoming queue when present. Captions remain above the seek rail while controls are visible. Fullscreen preserves custom controls where the browser supports element fullscreen. An owned next episode can appear in a dismissible end card above the control region. Acceptance: no fabricated chapters, unavailable episodes, or nonfunctional capability buttons appear; tools and end cards leave seek and transport usable. Maps to WP2; visual references R13 through R16.

8. **The player feels like one uninterrupted experience.** Expanding, phone collapse/re-expand, desktop presentation changes, opening tools, entering fullscreen, or opening/closing the popup's native window never restarts media or changes speed, position, queue order, bookmarks, or the sleep deadline. Utility and transport buttons use icons with state-aware tooltips and accessible names. Lists, tabs, lyric lines, headings, and time information keep readable text. Acceptance: the same playback asset and request generation survive presentation changes; only an explicit playback action, or the desktop/tablet dock's Close player action, changes the session. Phone Collapse changes only presentation; the popup has no in-player exit. Maps to all work packages, with integrated proof in WP6.

Representative journeys:

- While browsing Listen, start a song, open Queue, remove one upcoming track, and switch to History. The page stays stationary and the current track keeps playing. Expand Now Playing and choose Lyrics from the dock; the built-in panel changes tabs.
- On a phone, expand the compact dock into the full player, tap the top-left downward chevron, and browse the library while music continues. Expand again to the same track and live position. For an audiobook, the armed sleep deadline and bookmarks remain intact through the same journey.
- Resume an audiobook, arm a thirty-minute sleep timer, open the popup, choose a chapter, and close the popup. The main player retains the authoritative timer and chapter position. Opening a bookmark still uses the existing captured-position dialog.
- Start an owned TV episode, wait for controls to hide, then open Episodes and choose another owned episode. At the end, dismiss Next episode to watch the remaining credits; playback still follows the existing natural-end policy.

Tuvima's global navigation, lane navigation, Browse pages, ingestion sidebar, permissions, and playback ownership remain in place. The mockups' sidebar, search bar, bell, Listen banner, podcasts, and invented sample content are outside scope. Also excluded are new casting/device services, native clients, snapshot performance redesign, trickplay/frame thumbnails, Document PiP, and reader or album-detail redesign.

## Architecture and behavior contracts

### Playback ownership and commands

`PlaybackSessionController` remains the transport/session owner. The `<audio>` stays in `ListenNowPlayingBar`; `<video>` stays in `VideoPlaybackHost`. Both remain mounted in `MainLayout`. New full-player and tool components never own a media element, load a stream, or restore a session just to render UI.

Introduce `IPlaybackCommandSink` with direct and broadcast implementations. Its capability matrix covers play/pause, next/previous, relative skip, seek, volume/mute, shuffle/repeat, chapter selection/stepping, queue selection/removal/clear, music and audiobook history, speed, authoritative sleep selection, and bookmarks. The desktop/tablet dock retains its existing explicit session-stop path; it is not an action exposed by `PlaybackFullPlayer`. Tab selection, sheet visibility, and phone Collapse are local presentation actions. The shared full player accepts an optional phone-only `OnCollapse` presentation callback; the phone supplies it and the popup does not. That callback reveals the existing browse surface/compact dock and preserves its scroll context; it never sends a transport Stop/Close/Pause command. The popup wires no player Close, Stop, Collapse, Back-to-dock, or window-close callbacks, menu actions, or keyboard handlers. Native popup window lifecycle may perform passive subscription/state cleanup without stopping playback or invoking `window.close()`. Favorite and More use existing authorized services/menu behavior with current-item guards.

Reuse `ListenPlaybackCommandDto`, `IListenPlaybackCommandChannel`, and `ListenPlaybackCommandOwner`. Keep recipient/owner generation, command deduplication, expected asset/request version, bookmark draft leases, unknown-outcome handling, and native sleep arms intact. If a missing operation requires a DTO addition, update its owner handler and contract tests together. Lyric API reads/writes belong in a shared, authorized lyrics presenter; lyric seeking goes through the sink. Inspection during implementation confirmed that the current snapshot/channel carries no selected lyric version, while popup selection is local to its separate circuit. WP4/WP5 therefore need a small presentation-only selection projection/event on the existing owner channel to satisfy walkthrough change 4 across windows. Bind it to owner, profile, asset and playback generation, accept only a track in the current authorized track list, and retain it across presentation changes. Clear it on subject/profile/access loss. Do not conflate this temporary choice with the explicit saved preferred-version action or create another transport owner.

Popup loss of owner, stale state, profile change, or revoked access produces a recoverable unavailable state and rejects stale commands. The popup never falls back to owning audio. Closing its window is distinct from the player's explicit Stop action.

Identity links and item-menu navigation in the popup must stay on the popup route and ask the existing main owner to perform normal app navigation. Inspection confirmed that current same-window popup anchors can enter `MainLayout` and mount another audio host. Use a guarded presentation-navigation callback/message for canonical local routes, tied to the displayed owner/profile/asset/request generation. The main host uses `NavigationManager` without a forced page load; assigning the opener's `location` would restart the main media host and is unsuitable. This preserves walkthrough changes 5 and 8 without adding a popup exit action or another transport owner.

Close player on the desktop/tablet dock is a session-stop action, not Pause or a presentation change. Route it through the existing stop/cleanup path once, retain saved resume/history/bookmarks, and dismiss obsolete tool UI. Reserve at least a 44px target at those docks' trailing end; compress identity or move secondary utilities into overflow before removing Close. Phone playback does not expose that session-stop action, including its compact dock. Its full player exposes only Collapse player, a 44px-or-larger top-left chevron that returns to browsing/mini dock and restores focus to the originating Expand control where available. Collapse dismisses temporary tools, retains the selected tab, and leaves the media element, live position, asset/request generation, rate, queue, bookmarks, and sleep deadline untouched. In the popup, omit the control and its callback entirely. Sheet dismissal closes only the sheet; popup native window closure leaves the main session running. Escape dismisses the top tool first and never stops playback or invokes window closure. Test phone collapse/re-expand, tool dismissal, dock Close, and native popup closure independently.

### Panels and icon controls

Create `PlaybackPopover` and one scoped coordinator for transient playback tools, including More and Speed/Sleep menus. Inline Now Playing tab state remains in `ListenContextSidebarState`; open/pinned/hover state is local presentation state, not a continuously persisted profile preference. Preserve the preferred tab per profile/media context and existing race protection. Opening an inline tab closes a transient panel; expanding/collapsing closes temporary panels and retains the selected tab.

Desktop panels open above their trigger with an 8px gap and an 8px viewport margin. Fine-pointer hover opens after 120ms and closes after a 300ms grace period spanning trigger and panel. Click/tap pins; a second click, Escape, or outside activation closes. Keyboard activation opens and focuses the appropriate panel content. Escape restores trigger focus; outside activation preserves the user's intended new focus. Hover alone never moves focus. Desktop panels are non-modal dialogs with names and `aria-expanded`/`aria-controls` on triggers. At 720px or narrower, use `PlaybackToolSheet` with a modal backdrop, focus trap, Escape, and focus restoration.

Reposition on scroll, resize, zoom, and content changes. Position against the active fullscreen root when applicable so panels are visible in fullscreen and escape transformed/overflow ancestors. Keep one content scroller. Dispose timers, observers, and listeners on close, item change, and host disposal.

Use shared `--playback-*` tokens. Ordinary panels are 400px wide, Lyrics 460px, and video context 440px, all clamped to available space. Maximum height is the lesser of 70dvh and 640px, further bounded by available space above the dock/control. Use 10px corners, a 1px playback border, the proposed translucent surface with 16px blur and an opaque fallback, no arrow, and a 160ms fade with a 6px rise. Reduced motion removes the rise.

All transport/utility controls use the shared 22px outline glyphs and targets of at least 44px. Extend `PlaybackIconButton` and the existing `AppSelect.TriggerContent` path; keep Speed and Sleep as flat shared selects. Remove visible control labels, values, and badges across dock, expanded, phone, popup, and video surfaces. Provide one shared tooltip implementation: 400ms pointer delay, immediate keyboard focus, viewport bounds, and suppression while its tool is open. The accessible name describes the same current state. Real toggles expose both `aria-pressed="true"` and `"false"`; selected tabs use tab semantics. Accent state also has a discernible shape/boundary treatment.

### Video context and lifecycle

Repair manifest propagation so both freshly fetched and supplied video manifests preserve normalized real chapters. Test both start paths, not only the line cited in the attachment. Chapters are shown as a context choice only with at least two real entries; chapter ticks and segment shading derive solely from verified manifest data.
